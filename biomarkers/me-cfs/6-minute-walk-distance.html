6-minute walk distance in ME/CFS

6-minute walk distance, also described as Functional capacity, is a functional marker reported as reduced in ME/CFS (ME/CFS, CFS) when patients are compared with healthy controls. Symptom domains tied to this marker in the source include activity limitation. The evidence summarised here comes from Maksoud et al. 2023 and reflects averages across cohorts rather than any single patient's result.

Frequently asked questions

Is 6-minute walk distance high or low in ME/CFS?

Reduced. Studies summarised in the OSMF atlas report lower 6-minute walk distance in ME/CFS than in healthy controls. Source: Maksoud et al. 2023.

What symptoms is 6-minute walk distance associated with in ME/CFS?

The cited literature associates this marker with Activity limitation. These are population-level associations and do not mean the marker causes the symptoms.

Which test measures 6-minute walk distance?

It is measured as a clinical or physiological test. Many of the cited studies used research-grade assays, so clinical availability may vary.

Is one abnormal 6-minute walk distance result diagnostic of ME/CFS?

No. No single biomarker is diagnostic of ME/CFS. 6-minute walk distance findings come from group comparisons, overlap considerably between patients and controls, and vary with study design, timing and comparison group. A result should be interpreted by a clinician alongside symptoms, history and other tests.

Is 6-minute walk distance specific to ME/CFS?

No. The same marker is also reported in Gulf War Illness, PACVS in this atlas, so it reflects shared biology (for example inflammation or vascular stress) rather than a condition-specific signature.
