Serotonin in Migraine

In Migraine, published studies report lower levels of Serotonin than in healthy state (during migraine attack vs. baseline/non-migraine). The cited literature links this alteration to migraine attack. The evidence summarised here comes from Villalón et al. 2003 and reflects averages across cohorts rather than any single patient's result.

Frequently asked questions

Is Serotonin high or low in Migraine?

Reduced. Studies summarised in the OSMF atlas report lower Serotonin in Migraine than in healthy state (during migraine attack vs. baseline/non-migraine). Source: Villalón et al. 2003.

What symptoms is Serotonin associated with in Migraine?

The cited literature associates this marker with migraine attack. These are population-level associations and do not mean the marker causes the symptoms.

Which test measures Serotonin?

The atlas record does not list a standardised clinical test code (LOINC) for Serotonin, which usually means it was measured with research assays or study-specific protocols rather than a routine clinical panel. Check the cited source for the exact method.

Is one abnormal Serotonin result diagnostic of Migraine?

No. No single biomarker is diagnostic of Migraine. Serotonin findings come from group comparisons, overlap considerably between patients and controls, and vary with study design, timing and comparison group. A result should be interpreted by a clinician alongside symptoms, history and other tests.

Is Serotonin specific to Migraine?

No. The same marker is also reported in Long COVID in this atlas, so it reflects shared biology (for example inflammation or vascular stress) rather than a condition-specific signature.
