Glutamate in Migraine

Glutamate belongs to the other group of Migraine markers and is reported to be raised relative to healthy controls (implied by 'migraine patients'). Clinically, it has been associated with migraine. The finding is drawn from D'Andrea et al. 2010; it describes group-level differences, not a threshold that classifies an individual.

Frequently asked questions

Is Glutamate high or low in Migraine?

Elevated. Studies summarised in the OSMF atlas report higher Glutamate in Migraine than in healthy controls (implied by 'migraine patients'). Source: D'Andrea et al. 2010.

What symptoms is Glutamate associated with in Migraine?

The cited literature associates this marker with migraine. These are population-level associations and do not mean the marker causes the symptoms.

Which test measures Glutamate?

The atlas record does not list a standardised clinical test code (LOINC) for Glutamate, which usually means it was measured with research assays or study-specific protocols rather than a routine clinical panel. Check the cited source for the exact method.

Is one abnormal Glutamate result diagnostic of Migraine?

No. No single biomarker is diagnostic of Migraine. Glutamate findings come from group comparisons, overlap considerably between patients and controls, and vary with study design, timing and comparison group. A result should be interpreted by a clinician alongside symptoms, history and other tests.
