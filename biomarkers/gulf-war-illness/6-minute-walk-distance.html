6-minute walk distance in Gulf War Illness

6-minute walk distance, also described as Functional capacity, is a functional marker reported as reduced in Gulf War Illness (GWI, Gulf War Syndrome) when patients are compared with healthy Gulf War veteran controls. The cited literature links this alteration to physical limitation. The evidence summarised here comes from Gean et al. 2021 and reflects averages across cohorts rather than any single patient's result.

Frequently asked questions

Is 6-minute walk distance high or low in Gulf War Illness?

Reduced. Studies summarised in the OSMF atlas report lower 6-minute walk distance in Gulf War Illness than in healthy Gulf War veteran controls. Source: Gean et al. 2021.

What symptoms is 6-minute walk distance associated with in Gulf War Illness?

The cited literature associates this marker with Physical limitation. These are population-level associations and do not mean the marker causes the symptoms.

Which test measures 6-minute walk distance?

It is measured as a clinical or physiological test. Many of the cited studies used research-grade assays, so clinical availability may vary.

Is one abnormal 6-minute walk distance result diagnostic of Gulf War Illness?

No. No single biomarker is diagnostic of Gulf War Illness. 6-minute walk distance findings come from group comparisons, overlap considerably between patients and controls, and vary with study design, timing and comparison group. A result should be interpreted by a clinician alongside symptoms, history and other tests.

Is 6-minute walk distance specific to Gulf War Illness?

No. The same marker is also reported in ME/CFS, PACVS in this atlas, so it reflects shared biology (for example inflammation or vascular stress) rather than a condition-specific signature.
