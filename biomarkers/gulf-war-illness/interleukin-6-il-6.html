Interleukin-6 (IL-6) in Gulf War Illness

Interleukin-6 (IL-6), also described as Pro-inflammatory cytokine, is a inflammatory marker reported as elevated in Gulf War Illness (GWI, Gulf War Syndrome) when patients are compared with healthy Gulf War veteran controls. Symptom domains tied to this marker in the source include systemic inflammation. The evidence summarised here comes from Johnson et al. 2016 and reflects averages across cohorts rather than any single patient's result.

Frequently asked questions

Is Interleukin-6 (IL-6) high or low in Gulf War Illness?

Elevated. Studies summarised in the OSMF atlas report higher Interleukin-6 (IL-6) in Gulf War Illness than in healthy Gulf War veteran controls. Source: Johnson et al. 2016.

What symptoms is Interleukin-6 (IL-6) associated with in Gulf War Illness?

The cited literature associates this marker with Systemic inflammation. These are population-level associations and do not mean the marker causes the symptoms.

Which test measures Interleukin-6 (IL-6)?

It is measured as a blood test (serum / plasma / whole blood). The standard laboratory code is LOINC 26881-5. Commercial laboratories such as Quest Diagnostics, LabCorp, Ulta Lab Tests list an orderable assay.

Is one abnormal Interleukin-6 (IL-6) result diagnostic of Gulf War Illness?

No. No single biomarker is diagnostic of Gulf War Illness. Interleukin-6 (IL-6) findings come from group comparisons, overlap considerably between patients and controls, and vary with study design, timing and comparison group. A result should be interpreted by a clinician alongside symptoms, history and other tests.

Is Interleukin-6 (IL-6) specific to Gulf War Illness?

No. The same marker is also reported in Atrial Fibrillation, Epilepsy, Long COVID, Low Back Pain, Lyme Disease, ME/CFS, Major Depressive Disorder, Migraine, PACVS, Rheumatoid Arthritis in this atlas, so it reflects shared biology (for example inflammation or vascular stress) rather than a condition-specific signature.
