Anti-STAB1 autoantibodies in PACVS

Anti-STAB1 autoantibodies, also described as Stabilin-1, is a autoimmune marker reported as elevated in PACVS (PACVS, PCVS) when patients are compared with healthy controls. The cited literature links this alteration to skin edema, rash. The finding is drawn from Mantovani et al. 2024; it describes group-level differences, not a threshold that classifies an individual.

Frequently asked questions

Is Anti-STAB1 autoantibodies high or low in PACVS?

Elevated. Studies summarised in the OSMF atlas report higher Anti-STAB1 autoantibodies in PACVS than in healthy controls. Source: Mantovani et al. 2024.

What symptoms is Anti-STAB1 autoantibodies associated with in PACVS?

The cited literature associates this marker with Skin edema, rash. These are population-level associations and do not mean the marker causes the symptoms.

Which test measures Anti-STAB1 autoantibodies?

It is measured as a blood test (serum / plasma / whole blood). Many of the cited studies used research-grade assays, so clinical availability may vary.

Is one abnormal Anti-STAB1 autoantibodies result diagnostic of PACVS?

No. No single biomarker is diagnostic of PACVS. Anti-STAB1 autoantibodies findings come from group comparisons, overlap considerably between patients and controls, and vary with study design, timing and comparison group. A result should be interpreted by a clinician alongside symptoms, history and other tests.
