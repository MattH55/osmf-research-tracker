Summary

Diethylstilbestrol has reached the approved stage (maximum clinical phase 4) for at least one indication, so its use in Prostate Cancer would be drug repurposing rather than first-in-human development. This does not mean it is approved for Prostate Cancer. ClinicalTrials.gov lists 5 registered trials linking Diethylstilbestrol to Prostate Cancer: 5 have completed. The most advanced is Phase 3 (NCT00699751). It plans or enrolled 921 participants. Registration activity spans 2002 to 2020. The literature layer holds 10 publications for this pair: 3 Cochrane reviews, 2 randomised controlled trial publications and 5 clinical trial publications. Publication years run from 1999 to 2012. Because at least one synthesis-level source exists (systematic review, meta-analysis or Cochrane review), this pair has been assessed beyond single studies, although the synthesis may concern a different indication.

The RepurpOS disease-intelligence file for Prostate Cancer ranks 50 candidate therapeutics from Open Targets, ChEMBL, DGIdb and PubMed; only a minority carry direct clinical evidence, and this page covers one of those. Spontaneous remission context recorded for the condition: Low-grade (Gleason 6/Grade Group 1): active surveillance appropriate in 40-50% — many will never need treatment; high-grade: lethal without treatment within 5-10 years.

How the evidence score is calculated
  • Each registered trial scores by status (recruiting / active / enrolling 3, completed 2.5, not yet recruiting 2, unknown 1, terminated / withdrawn / suspended 0.5) plus a phase bonus (phase 3-4 +2, phase 2 +1, phase 1 +0.5). The trial component is capped at 30.
  • Each literature item scores by design (Cochrane review 5, meta-analysis 4, systematic review 3, RCT 3, clinical trial publication 2, curated reference 1.5, other PubMed record 1). The literature component is capped at 30.
  • The existing evidence tier adds 15 (A / Strong), 10 (B / Moderate), 5 (C / Preliminary) or 0 (D / Anecdotal).
  • Agents approved for any indication (max clinical phase 4) add 5, because an approved agent has an established safety profile that lowers the barrier to repurposing trials.
  • The score ranks what has been studied, not what works. It does not read effect sizes or directions of effect.
