Summary

Adalimumab has reached the approved stage (maximum clinical phase 4) for at least one indication, so its use in Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) would be drug repurposing rather than first-in-human development. This does not mean it is approved for Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE). ClinicalTrials.gov lists 1 registered trial linking Adalimumab to Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE): 1 is active or not yet recruiting. The most advanced is Phase 2 (NCT07138898). It plans or enrolled 80 participants. Registered activity dates to 2025. The literature layer holds 5 publications for this pair: 1 Cochrane review, 3 systematic reviews and 1 clinical trial publication. Publication years run from 2007 to 2025. Because at least one synthesis-level source exists (systematic review, meta-analysis or Cochrane review), this pair has been assessed beyond single studies, although the synthesis may concern a different indication.

The RepurpOS disease-intelligence file for Systemic Lupus Erythematosus ranks 276 candidate therapeutics from Open Targets, ChEMBL, DGIdb and PubMed; only a minority carry direct clinical evidence, and this page covers one of those. Spontaneous remission context recorded for the condition: Spontaneous drug-free remission: 2-10% in some cohorts over 5 years; disease-free survival in 15-20% at 20 years; renal lupus (LN) progression to ESRD in 10-20% within 20 years on standard care.

How the evidence score is calculated
  • Each registered trial scores by status (recruiting / active / enrolling 3, completed 2.5, not yet recruiting 2, unknown 1, terminated / withdrawn / suspended 0.5) plus a phase bonus (phase 3-4 +2, phase 2 +1, phase 1 +0.5). The trial component is capped at 30.
  • Each literature item scores by design (Cochrane review 5, meta-analysis 4, systematic review 3, RCT 3, clinical trial publication 2, curated reference 1.5, other PubMed record 1). The literature component is capped at 30.
  • The existing evidence tier adds 15 (A / Strong), 10 (B / Moderate), 5 (C / Preliminary) or 0 (D / Anecdotal).
  • Agents approved for any indication (max clinical phase 4) add 5, because an approved agent has an established safety profile that lowers the barrier to repurposing trials.
  • The score ranks what has been studied, not what works. It does not read effect sizes or directions of effect.
