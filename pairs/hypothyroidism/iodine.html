Iodine for Hypothyroidism: evidence, trials and status

Summary

Iodine has reached Preclinical as its most advanced recorded development stage across any indication, and the database holds no approval for Hypothyroidism. ClinicalTrials.gov lists 8 registered trials linking Iodine to Hypothyroidism: 1 is currently recruiting; 6 have completed; 1 is terminated, withdrawn, suspended or of unknown status. The most advanced is Phase 4 (NCT02280330). The largest enrolment is 6564 participants (NCT03180892). Registration activity spans 2011 to 2021. The literature layer holds 19 publications for this pair: 5 Cochrane reviews, 4 meta-analyses, 3 systematic reviews, 2 randomised controlled trial publications and 5 clinical trial publications. Publication years run from 2014 to 2026. Because at least one synthesis-level source exists (systematic review, meta-analysis or Cochrane review), this pair has been assessed beyond single studies, although the synthesis may concern a different indication.

The RepurpOS disease-intelligence file for Hypothyroidism ranks 81 candidate therapeutics from Open Targets, ChEMBL, DGIdb and PubMed; only a minority carry direct clinical evidence, and this page covers one of those. Spontaneous remission context recorded for the condition: Hashimoto's (most common cause) is irreversible in majority; spontaneous recovery in <5%; subclinical hypothyroidism spontaneously normalizes in ~30-40% of cases within 2 years without treatment.

How the evidence score is calculated
  • Each registered trial scores by status (recruiting / active / enrolling 3, completed 2.5, not yet recruiting 2, unknown 1, terminated / withdrawn / suspended 0.5) plus a phase bonus (phase 3-4 +2, phase 2 +1, phase 1 +0.5). The trial component is capped at 30.
  • Each literature item scores by design (Cochrane review 5, meta-analysis 4, systematic review 3, RCT 3, clinical trial publication 2, curated reference 1.5, other PubMed record 1). The literature component is capped at 30.
  • The existing evidence tier adds 15 (A / Strong), 10 (B / Moderate), 5 (C / Preliminary) or 0 (D / Anecdotal).
  • Agents approved for any indication (max clinical phase 4) add 5, because an approved agent has an established safety profile that lowers the barrier to repurposing trials.
  • The score ranks what has been studied, not what works. It does not read effect sizes or directions of effect.
