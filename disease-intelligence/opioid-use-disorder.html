Remission & chronicity

Spontaneous remission
Spontaneous recovery (without MOUD) in 20-30% of OUD patients over 10 years; but overdose mortality risk during active disorder is extreme (5-10 per 1000 person-years); relapse rate 90%+ in first year after discontinuing MOUD
Best-intervention remission
Buprenorphine maintenance: 50-70% retention in treatment at 1 year vs <20% for placebo; naltrexone extended-release (Vivitrol): comparable to buprenorphine when initiated; methadone: superior retention but restricted distribution; overdose mortality reduced 50-70% with any MOUD

Primary barrier
access/policy
Barrier detail: US eliminated the DEA X-waiver requirement for buprenorphine prescribing in December 2022 (a major policy win) but prescribing rates have increased slowly; stigma remains; methadone access restricted to OTPs (clinics); harm reduction infrastructure (fentanyl test strips, naloxone) still insufficiently deployed; 80% of people with OUD receive no treatment

Last SoC change: 2023 — DEA X-waiver elimination fully implemented; 2022 federal legislation allowing take-home methadone expanded; 2021 FDA approved injectable buprenorphine (Sublocade) for once-monthly dosing; buprenorphine SL since 2002; methadone for OUD since 1964
