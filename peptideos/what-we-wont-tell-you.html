Editorial policy

What we won’t tell you

PeptideOS answers three questions about each compound: what the human evidence shows, what its legal status is, and what is actually in the vial. It does not answer the questions below, on any page, in any format.

Doses

No dose amounts appear anywhere on this site, including the doses printed on approved labels. Approved drugs have a label and a prescriber; for everything else, a number on this page would be the first line of a usage guide.

Why: A dose table is the single line that turns an information resource into an instruction manual for unapproved drugs.

How to use it

Nothing about reconstitution, injection, cycling, stacking, timing or storage.

Why: Usage guidance is the rest of the instruction manual.

Where to get it

No vendor names, links, rankings or “reputable source” lists. Vendors will appear only as the subjects of published purity testing, with passes and failures.

Why: The existing peptide content ecosystem is paid for by sales. A resource that earns nothing when someone buys is the only kind that can be believed about whether the product works.

Whether you should take it

No recommendations, no second-person advice, no testimonials or before-and-after stories.

Why: That decision belongs to a person and their clinician, with the evidence in front of them. Anecdote is not evidence, and it is the main form of marketing in this market.

That a mechanism means it works

Mechanisms are described last on every page, in neutral language, with the model named (“in rats”, “in cultured cells”).

Why: A plausible mechanism reads like evidence to a non-specialist and costs nothing to assert.

How this is enforced

These rules are code, not intentions. The build runs a linter over every record and refuses to publish one that contains a dose amount, administration language, a vendor or purchase phrase, second-person advice, a testimonial, or a benefit claim without a named model. The rules and the linter are public.

If you need dosing information for an approved medicine, it is on the official label and comes from your prescriber or pharmacist.

Conflict of interest. PeptideOS is published by the Open Source Medicine Foundation. No peptide vendor, manufacturer, compounding pharmacy or supplement company funds any part of it, and it carries no advertising or affiliate links. Full disclosure.
