Setmelanotide

Cyclic octapeptide: Ac-Arg-cyclo[Cys-D-Ala-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Cys]-NH2 (disulfide between the two cysteines). Contains D-amino acids, so no plain one-letter sequence is given. Structure as described in the US prescribing information (chemical name: acetyl-L-arginyl-L-cysteinyl-D-alanyl-L-histidinyl-D-phenylalanyl-L-arginyl-L-tryptophanyl-L-cysteinamide cyclic (2-8)-disulfide).

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Codes: RM-493 · Brands: Imcivree

A synthetic cyclic peptide that activates the melanocortin-4 receptor, a brain receptor in the leptin-melanocortin pathway that regulates hunger. It is approved, under the brand Imcivree, only for specific rare forms of obesity: obesity due to POMC, PCSK1 or LEPR deficiency, Bardet-Biedl syndrome, and acquired hypothalamic obesity (approved in the US, EU, UK and Japan). The US label states it is not expected to be effective in general (polygenic) obesity.

02What the human evidence shows

E2

Acquired hypothalamic obesity

1published human RCTs
2published human trials
27registered studies
14with posted results

An open-label phase 2 trial in 18 patients reported that 16 reached at least 5% BMI reduction at 16 weeks. A double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial (TRANSCEND, about 140 enrolled) has been presented as a conference abstract and its results are posted on ClinicalTrials.gov; a full peer-reviewed publication was not found on the retrieval date. By the retrieval date the indication was approved in the US (March 2026), Japan (August 2026), the EU and the UK.

  • [1] 16 of 18 patients reached at least 5% BMI reduction after 16 weeks in an open-label trial
  • [2] Conference abstract reporting the randomised placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in acquired hypothalamic obesity

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “setmelanotide”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E3

Obesity in Bardet-Biedl syndrome (and Alström syndrome)

1published human RCTs
1published human trials
27registered studies
14with posted results

One phase 3 trial (38 randomised) had a 14-week double-blind placebo-controlled period followed by 52 weeks of open-label treatment. Its primary endpoint was single-arm: 32.3% of patients aged 12 or older with Bardet-Biedl syndrome reached at least 10% weight reduction after 52 weeks. The randomised period was short and small, so the controlled evidence is limited. Alström syndrome was included in the trial but is not an approved indication.

  • [3] 32.3% of patients aged 12 or older with Bardet-Biedl syndrome reached at least 10% weight reduction after 52 weeks

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “setmelanotide”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E4

Obesity due to POMC, PCSK1 or LEPR deficiency

No randomised controlled trial in humans has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
3published human trials
27registered studies
14with posted results

Evidence comes from two single-arm, open-label phase 3 trials (10 and 11 participants in the published full analysis sets) with a short blinded placebo-withdrawal sequence, and an earlier two-patient open-label report. Eight of 10 participants with POMC deficiency and 5 of 11 with LEPR deficiency reached at least 10% weight loss at about one year. There is no parallel-group randomised comparison, which is typical for ultra-rare conditions but limits the strength of inference.

  • [4] Weight loss of at least 10% at about one year in single-arm trials of people with POMC or LEPR deficiency obesity
  • [5] Reduced hunger and substantial weight loss in two patients with POMC deficiency in an open-label study

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “setmelanotide”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S2Substantial controlled-trial safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

  • Skin hyperpigmentation, darkening of pre-existing naevi and new melanocytic naevi [6]
    Listed in US prescribing information (Warnings and Precautions 5.4); skin hyperpigmentation is among the most common adverse reactions.
  • Disturbance in sexual arousal, including spontaneous penile erection [6]
    US prescribing information, Warnings and Precautions 5.1; spontaneous penile erection listed among common adverse reactions.
  • Depression and suicidal ideation [6]
    US prescribing information, Warnings and Precautions 5.2.
  • Acute adrenal insufficiency and sodium imbalance in patients with acquired hypothalamic obesity [6]
    Added to US prescribing information in March 2026 (Warnings and Precautions 5.5 and 5.6).
  • Gastrointestinal effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain) and injection-site reactions [6]
    Most common adverse reactions in US prescribing information section 6.1.

04What is unknown

  • No parallel-group randomised trial in POMC, PCSK1 or LEPR deficiency; efficacy rests on single-arm data in 21 people in the two pivotal trials
  • Long-term (multi-year) outcomes beyond weight and hunger, such as cardiometabolic events, are not established
  • Long-term consequences of persistent skin hyperpigmentation and new melanocytic naevi are not characterised
  • Peer-reviewed full publication of the phase 3 acquired hypothalamic obesity trial was not found on the retrieval date
  • Efficacy in general (polygenic) obesity is not established; the US label states it would not be expected to be effective

07Mechanism

Setmelanotide is an agonist of the melanocortin-4 (MC4) receptor, part of the hypothalamic leptin-melanocortin pathway that regulates hunger and energy expenditure; it is intended to act downstream of genetic or acquired defects in that pathway.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
