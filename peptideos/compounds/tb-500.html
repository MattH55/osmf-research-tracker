Class C · Gray market

TB-500

LKKTETQ

Seven residues corresponding to positions 17-23 of mature thymosin beta-4 (the actin-binding region). An anti-doping analysis identified the N-terminally acetylated form (Ac-LKKTETQ) in a product sold as TB-500; FDA names the substance 'thymosin beta-4, fragment (LKKTETQ), also known as TB-500'. The content of other products sold under this name was not assessed.

Also: TB500, Thymosin beta-4 fragment (LKKTETQ), Ac-LKKTETQ

A synthetic seven-amino-acid fragment of thymosin beta-4, a 43-residue protein found in most human cells. It is often marketed as if it were thymosin beta-4 itself, with claims of faster recovery from tendon, ligament and muscle injuries and wound healing. Research on the full-length protein does not transfer automatically to the fragment. TB-500 is not approved as a medicine in any jurisdiction checked. FDA states it has identified no human exposure data for it, and the World Anti-Doping Agency names it as prohibited.

This row concerns full-length thymosin beta-4, a different molecule from TB-500, and is shown because marketing cites this research. A double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-escalation phase 2 trial of topical thymosin beta-4 in 73 patients with venous leg ulcers across eight European sites reported safety comparable to placebo; the efficacy findings were described only as suggestive, and no confirmatory trial was identified. No trial has tested injected full-length thymosin beta-4 for tendon, ligament or muscle injury.

  • [1] Topical full-length thymosin beta-4 was tolerated similarly to placebo in 73 patients with venous ulcers; efficacy reported as suggestive, not confirmed
  • [2] Full-length thymosin beta-4 increased wound re-epithelialisation and contraction in a rat full-thickness wound model

The only efficacy study of the fragment identified was in mice: the synthetic seven-residue peptide LKKTETQ gave dermal wound-repair results in aged mice comparable to full-length thymosin beta-4. No study of the fragment in tendon, ligament or muscle injury models was identified. No published human trial of TB-500 exists. A phase 1/2 trial of the fragment for cardiovascular biomarkers was registered in China in 2026 and is recruiting, with no results; it does not address injury recovery.

  • [3] The synthetic fragment LKKTETQ increased dermal wound closure in aged mice to a degree comparable with full-length thymosin beta-4

03Safety signals

S5No human safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

04What is unknown

  • No published human data on TB-500 of any kind: no pharmacokinetics, no safety and no efficacy by any route
  • No study of the fragment in tendon, ligament or muscle injury models was identified; claims for these uses rest on extrapolation from the full-length protein or from skin wounds in mice
  • Immunogenicity is unassessed; FDA states compounded TB-500 may pose a risk of immunogenicity due to aggregation and peptide-related impurities
  • What products sold as TB-500 actually contain (the acetylated fragment, the free fragment, full-length thymosin beta-4 or other material) was not assessed for this record
  • Regulatory status outside the US was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

TB-500 corresponds to the actin-binding region of thymosin beta-4, a protein that sequesters monomeric actin inside cells; in aged mice the free fragment increased dermal wound closure.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
