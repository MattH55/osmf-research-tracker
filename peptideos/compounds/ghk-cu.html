GHK-Cu

GHK

Copper(II) complex of the tripeptide glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine (GHK). The peptide occurs naturally in human plasma.

Also: Copper peptide GHK-Cu, Copper tripeptide, Glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine copper(II)

A copper complex of a three-amino-acid peptide found naturally in human plasma. It is a common ingredient in topical cosmetic skin products and is also sold on the gray market as an injectable with anti-ageing and tissue-healing claims. A topical gel was tested for diabetic foot ulcers in the 1990s; no approval of it was identified. It is not an FDA-approved medicine, and in the US FDA treats injectable and non-injectable use separately for compounding.

02What the human evidence shows

E3

Diabetic foot ulcers (topical gel)

1published human RCTs
1published human trials
3registered studies
0with posted results

A multicentre, randomised, evaluator-blinded, vehicle-controlled trial of a GHK-copper gel (Iamin Gel) in diabetic neuropathic plantar ulcers, published in 1994, reported greater median ulcer closure and fewer infections than vehicle when added to standard wound care. The number of participants was not stated in the abstract reviewed. No confirmatory trial was identified, and no approval of the gel was found.

  • [1] Greater median plantar ulcer closure and lower infection incidence than vehicle in a randomised, evaluator-blinded trial in patients with diabetes

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “GHK-Cu”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E3

Skin ageing, wrinkles and post-procedure skin (topical cosmetic)

1published human RCTs
1published human trials
3registered studies
0with posted results

Topical GHK-Cu is widely used in cosmetics, but controlled clinical data are sparse. In a small randomised study of 13 patients after carbon-dioxide laser resurfacing around the mouth, skin products with GHK-Cu did not reduce redness or improve blinded ratings of wrinkles or skin quality compared with products without it; only patient-reported satisfaction differed. Other published cosmetic studies mostly test multi-ingredient products, so the contribution of GHK-Cu cannot be isolated.

  • [2] No difference in erythema, wrinkles or blinded skin-quality ratings versus control regimen in 13 patients; higher patient-reported satisfaction

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “GHK-Cu”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E6

Injected GHK-Cu for tissue healing, inflammation or anti-ageing

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
0published human trials
3registered studies
0with posted results

No human study of injected GHK-Cu has been published. In rats, injections into the knee joint after ligament reconstruction improved knee laxity and graft stiffness at 6 weeks but not at 12 weeks. In mice with chemically induced colitis, GHK-Cu reduced inflammatory markers and colon damage. FDA states that there are limited data in humans to inform safety for injectable use.

  • [3] Smaller knee laxity difference at 6 weeks but no difference at 12 weeks after ACL reconstruction in rats
  • [4] Lower disease-activity scores and inflammatory cytokines in mice with DSS-induced colitis

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “GHK-Cu”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S3Limited human safety data
  • Regulator-identified risk for injectable use [5]
    FDA lists GHK-Cu for injectable routes among substances previously in 503A Category 2 whose nominations were withdrawn, citing immunogenicity risk from aggregation and peptide-related impurities and limited human data. This is a stated risk, not a documented case of harm.

04What is unknown

  • No published human study of injected GHK-Cu, for efficacy, pharmacokinetics or safety
  • How much topically applied GHK-Cu penetrates intact human skin is not established
  • The 1994 diabetic ulcer trial has not been independently replicated, and its full participant numbers were not verified for this record
  • Whether injected GHK-Cu alters copper balance or copper-related toxicity in humans has not been studied
  • Purity, copper content and aggregation of gray-market injectable products are uncharacterised; FDA cites aggregation and impurities as an immunogenicity concern
  • Regulatory status outside the US, including cosmetic-ingredient rules, was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

GHK forms a complex with copper(II) ions. In mice with chemically induced colitis GHK-Cu lowered inflammatory cytokines and increased SIRT1 protein expression; its molecular targets in human tissue are not established.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
