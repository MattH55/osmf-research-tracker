Class C · Gray market

CJC-1295

A modified GHRH(1-29) amide analogue with a C-terminal reactive group designed to bind covalently to circulating albumin (the developer's 'drug affinity complex'), which extended its half-life to several days in humans. A 29-residue amidated GHRH analogue without the albumin-binding group has been identified in material sold under the CJC-1295 name.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: CJC-1295 with DAC, CJC 1295 · Codes: CJC-1295

A synthetic, long-acting analogue of growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) developed by ConjuChem in the 2000s. Its only registered efficacy trial, in people with HIV and abdominal fat, was terminated in 2006 and never reported. It has no approval anywhere checked and no active sponsor. It is sold as a "research chemical" with claims of muscle gain, fat loss, better sleep and "anti-ageing". FDA has identified serious adverse events associated with it.

02What the human evidence shows

E3

Raising GH and IGF-1 levels (pharmacodynamic marker, healthy adults)

No randomised controlled trial in humans has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
2published human trials
1registered studies
0with posted results

Two randomised, placebo-controlled, ascending-dose studies in healthy adults (lasting 28 and 49 days) found dose-dependent rises in plasma GH and IGF-1 lasting days after a single dose, with an estimated half-life of about 6 to 8 days; no serious adverse reactions were reported. A separate uncontrolled before-and-after study in healthy young men found raised trough and mean GH with preserved pulsatility, and higher IGF-1, one week after a single dose. These are small, short studies of a hormone marker, not of any clinical outcome.

  • [1] Dose-dependent increases in plasma GH and IGF-1 lasting days in healthy adults in two randomised, placebo-controlled dose-escalation studies; estimated half-life 5.8 to 8.1 days
  • [2] Raised trough and mean GH and IGF-1 with preserved GH pulsatility one week after a single dose in healthy young men (uncontrolled)

Growth in GHRH deficiency

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
0published human trials
1registered studies
0with posted results

In mice genetically lacking GHRH, five weeks of treatment from one week of age normalised body weight, length and body composition when given every 24 hours, with lesser effects at longer intervals. This is a deficiency model; it does not show effects in animals or people with normal GHRH. No human growth study exists.

  • [3] Normalised body weight, length and body composition in GHRH-knockout mice

No published study in humans or animals has tested any of these outcomes. Published human studies measured only hormone levels (GH and IGF-1) in healthy adults over weeks (see the pharmacodynamic entry above). Raised hormone levels are a laboratory measure, not evidence of muscle gain, fat loss, better sleep or any health benefit.

  • [4] Product-identity finding, not efficacy: a seized preparation contained a 29-residue amidated peptide consistent with the sequence marketed as CJC-1295

Visceral fat in people with HIV

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human trials
1registered studies

A randomised placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial in 120 people with HIV-associated visceral obesity (NCT00267527, ConjuChem) began in December 2005 and is registered as terminated in 2006. No reason is given on the registry and no results were posted or published, so it is not counted as a published trial. No published study in humans or animals has tested CJC-1295 on visceral fat.

03Safety signals

S3Limited human safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

  • Increased heart rate and systemic vasodilatory reaction [5]
    FDA's compounding safety-risk summary states it has identified serious adverse events associated with CJC-1295, including increased heart rate and a systemic vasodilatory reaction, and that available clinical data are limited. The underlying case details are not published there.
  • Immunogenicity and impurity concerns [5]
    FDA states compounded CJC-1295 may pose a risk of immunogenicity for certain routes and raises peptide-impurity and characterisation concerns.

04What is unknown

  • No human efficacy data for any clinical outcome; the published human studies (two small randomised placebo-controlled studies and one uncontrolled study, all in healthy adults) measured only GH and IGF-1 and lasted under two months
  • No human safety data beyond short dose-finding studies in small numbers of healthy adults; no data on repeated exposure over months or years
  • Why the only efficacy trial (NCT00267527) was terminated is not stated on the registry, and its data were never reported
  • Effects of sustained elevation of GH and IGF-1 from a long-acting analogue on glucose metabolism, cancer risk or the heart are unstudied
  • Products sold as CJC-1295 may contain a different peptide: a 29-residue analogue without the albumin-binding group was identified in a seized preparation, so the half-life and effects of what is in a given vial are unknown
  • Regulatory status in the UK, Australia, Japan and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

CJC-1295 is a GHRH analogue designed to bind albumin after absorption, prolonging its action; in rats it activated pituitary GHRH receptors, and in healthy adults it raised GH and IGF-1 for several days.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
