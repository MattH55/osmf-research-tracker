MOTS-c

MRWQEMGYIFYPRKLR

16 residues encoded by a short open reading frame within the mitochondrial 12S rRNA gene (UniProt A0A0C5B5G6).

Also: Mitochondrial open reading frame of the 12S rRNA-c, Mitochondrial-derived peptide MOTS-c

A 16-amino-acid peptide encoded in mitochondrial DNA and found naturally in human blood and muscle. A synthetic version is sold on the gray market with claims of weight loss, better insulin sensitivity, exercise performance and longevity. It is not approved as a medicine in any jurisdiction checked. FDA states it has identified no human exposure data for products containing it by any route. The World Anti-Doping Agency names it as a prohibited metabolic modulator.

02What the human evidence shows

E6

Obesity, weight loss and insulin resistance

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
0published human trials
5registered studies
0with posted results

The founding study reported that MOTS-c treatment in mice prevented diet-induced obesity and age-related and diet-induced insulin resistance. No published human trial of MOTS-c for weight, glucose control or insulin sensitivity was identified. A placebo-controlled phase 2 trial in adults with prediabetes was registered in China in 2026 and is recruiting, with no results. A company-developed analogue (CB4211) completed a phase 1 trial, but that is a different molecule and its results were not identified in a peer-reviewed journal.

  • [1] MOTS-c treatment prevented diet-induced obesity and insulin resistance in mice

Exercise performance, physical capacity and ageing

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
0published human trials
5registered studies
0with posted results

In mice, MOTS-c treatment increased running performance in young, middle-aged and old animals, and treatment started late in life increased physical capacity. In humans, the same study measured only endogenous MOTS-c, which rose in muscle and blood with exercise; no human was given MOTS-c. No human trial of administered MOTS-c for exercise capacity, muscle function or ageing outcomes was identified.

  • [2] MOTS-c treatment increased running capacity in young, middle-aged and old mice; endogenous MOTS-c rose with exercise in humans

03Safety signals

S5No human safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

04What is unknown

  • No published human data of any kind on administered MOTS-c: no pharmacokinetics, no safety and no efficacy by any route
  • Immunogenicity of injected synthetic MOTS-c is unassessed; FDA states compounded MOTS-c may pose significant risk for immunogenicity and raises peptide-impurity and characterisation concerns
  • Whether findings in mice on weight, insulin resistance and running capacity apply to humans has not been tested; the first registered human efficacy trial had not reported at review
  • Results for the analogue CB4211 were not found in a peer-reviewed journal, and an analogue's effects cannot be assumed for MOTS-c itself
  • Regulatory status outside the US was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

MOTS-c is a peptide encoded in mitochondrial DNA; in mouse and cell studies it acted mainly on skeletal muscle, inhibiting the folate cycle and de novo purine synthesis and leading to activation of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK).

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
