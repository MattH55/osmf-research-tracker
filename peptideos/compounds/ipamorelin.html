Ipamorelin

Pentapeptide Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH2 (Aib, alpha-aminoisobutyric acid; D-2-Nal, D-2-naphthylalanine); contains non-standard and D-amino acids.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Ipamorelin acetate

A synthetic five-residue peptide that stimulates growth hormone release by acting on the ghrelin (growth hormone secretagogue) receptor. It was developed by Novo Nordisk in the 1990s and later tested by Helsinn as an intravenous treatment for postoperative ileus, where its published Phase 2 trial did not meet its efficacy endpoints. It is not approved anywhere checked and has no active sponsor. It is sold as a "research chemical" with claims of muscle gain, fat loss, recovery, sleep and "anti-ageing".

02What the human evidence shows

E3

Postoperative ileus

No randomised controlled trial in humans has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
2published human trials
3registered studies
0with posted results

In a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial in 114 adults after bowel resection, intravenous ipamorelin did not significantly shorten time to tolerating a solid meal and showed no differences on secondary endpoints. A second, larger Phase 2 trial (NCT01280344, 320 enrolled, completed 2014) has no results posted on the registry and no publication was identified. In a rat model of postoperative ileus, intravenous ipamorelin shortened time to first bowel movement.

  • [1] No significant difference from placebo in time to first tolerated meal in 114 bowel-resection patients
  • [2] Shortened time to first bowel movement after laparotomy and intestinal manipulation in rats

Bone growth and bone mineral content

Rat studies only. Ipamorelin increased longitudinal bone growth in young rats and bone mineral content in adult female rats; in the adult rats the gain reflected larger bones, with volumetric bone density unchanged. No human bone study exists.

  • [3] Increased longitudinal bone growth in rats
  • [4] Increased bone mineral content via larger bone dimensions, without change in volumetric density, in adult female rats

E8

Muscle gain, fat loss, recovery, sleep and anti-ageing (gray-market claims)

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
0published human trials
3registered studies
0with posted results

No human study has tested any of these outcomes. Human data are limited to a pharmacokinetic study in healthy men showing a single, short-lived rise in growth hormone after intravenous infusion. In mice, ipamorelin increased body fat rather than reducing it, an effect the authors attributed to GH-independent mechanisms such as increased feeding. Raised hormone levels are a laboratory measure, not evidence of muscle gain, fat loss or any health benefit.

  • [5] Single episode of GH release with a terminal half-life of about 2 hours after intravenous infusion in healthy men
  • [6] Increased relative body fat and fat pad weight in GH-deficient and GH-intact mice
  • [7] Product-identity finding, not efficacy: glycine-extended ipamorelin (Gly-ipamorelin) identified in black-market growth-promoting products

03Safety signals

S3Limited human safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

  • Serious adverse events including death in an intravenous gastric-motility trial [8]
    FDA's compounding safety-risk summary states that a published study identified serious adverse events including death when ipamorelin was given intravenously for gastric motility. The published postoperative ileus trial reported treatment-emergent adverse events in 87.5% on ipamorelin versus 94.8% on placebo, in a post-surgical population; causality for individual events is not established in either source.
  • Immunogenicity and impurity concerns [8]
    FDA states compounded ipamorelin acetate may pose a risk of immunogenicity for certain routes because of aggregation or peptide-related impurities, and that its unnatural amino acids complicate characterisation.

04What is unknown

  • No published human pharmacokinetic or safety data for subcutaneous use were identified in this review (published human studies used intravenous infusion); FDA states it has not identified safety-related information for certain other injectable routes
  • No human data on repeated exposure beyond about a week; no long-term safety data
  • Results of the larger Phase 2 ileus trial (NCT01280344, 320 enrolled) have not been posted or published
  • Whether it increases body fat in humans, as it did in mice, is unknown
  • Product identity: N-terminally glycine-extended ipamorelin (Gly-ipamorelin) has been identified in black-market growth-promoting products, so what a vial sold as ipamorelin contains is uncertain
  • Regulatory status in the UK, Australia, Japan and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Ipamorelin is an agonist at the growth hormone secretagogue (ghrelin) receptor; it released growth hormone in rats and swine, and in swine it did not raise ACTH or cortisol above levels seen with GHRH.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
