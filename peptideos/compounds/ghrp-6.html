GHRP-6

Synthetic hexapeptide His-D-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH2; contains D-amino acids and a C-terminal amide, so no standard one-letter sequence is given.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Growth hormone-releasing peptide-6, Growth hormone releasing hexapeptide · Codes: GHRP-6

A synthetic six-amino-acid peptide, one of the first growth hormone secretagogues, which acts on the ghrelin receptor to release growth hormone from the pituitary. It was studied widely in human endocrine physiology experiments in the 1990s but was never approved as a medicine in any jurisdiction checked. It is sold online as a research chemical with claims of raising growth hormone, appetite and muscle mass. A Cuban research centre has tested it with epidermal growth factor in acute stroke.

02What the human evidence shows

Growth hormone release

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Many small, short human pharmacology studies, several of them randomised placebo-controlled crossover designs in healthy volunteers or patients, show that intravenous GHRP-6 acutely raises blood growth hormone, an effect that is greater when combined with GHRH and partly depends on endogenous GHRH. It also raised ACTH and cortisol. These studies measured hormone levels over hours; none tested sustained IGF-I, body composition, strength or any clinical outcome. The number of such studies was not counted, so both counts are left blank.

  • [1] Acute growth hormone release in healthy men, synergistic with GHRH
  • [2] Maximal growth hormone response to GHRP-6 in humans required endogenous hypothalamic GHRH
  • [3] Overnight growth hormone, ACTH and cortisol rose and stage 2 sleep increased in healthy men compared with placebo

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “GHRP-6”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

The COURAGE-2 phase III trial in Cuba randomised 188 patients with acute ischaemic stroke to intravenous epidermal growth factor plus GHRP-6 or standard care, open-label. It missed its primary endpoint: no difference in disability (modified Rankin Scale), Barthel index or survival in the whole population. Subgroup analyses suggested benefit in severe stroke, which is hypothesis-generating only. The trial tested a combination given in hospital, so it does not isolate GHRP-6 and does not bear on self-administered use.

  • [4] No difference in disability, Barthel index or survival with EGF plus GHRP-6 versus standard care in patients with acute ischaemic stroke; primary endpoint missed

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “growth hormone releasing peptide 6”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S3Limited human safety data
  • Acute rise in ACTH and cortisol [3]
    In healthy men, repeated intravenous GHRP-6 raised overnight ACTH and cortisol compared with placebo.
  • Cortisol effects and reduced insulin sensitivity [5]
    FDA's compounding safety summary states that the limited available data reveal concerns including effects on cortisol and increased blood glucose from decreased insulin sensitivity.
  • Serious adverse events in a stroke trial, not attributed to treatment [4]
    In COURAGE-2, serious adverse events occurred in 30 of 95 patients given EGF plus GHRP-6 and 18 of 93 on standard care; investigators judged none treatment-related.

04What is unknown

  • No human study of muscle mass, strength or body composition with GHRP-6 was identified
  • No human data on repeated use over weeks or months outside hospital were identified; most human exposure is single sessions lasting hours
  • Whether the growth hormone response persists or attenuates with continued use was not established for GHRP-6 in the sources reviewed (it attenuated partly with the related peptide hexarelin)
  • Effects of repeated rises in cortisol and of reduced insulin sensitivity over time are unstudied
  • Australian status was not verified from the TGA register; GHRP-6 is named in Schedule 4 and Appendix D of the Poisons Standard (October 2026, F2026L01327), which controls supply but is not a product approval
  • Status in the UK, Japan and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

GHRP-6 is an agonist of the growth hormone secretagogue receptor (the ghrelin receptor); in human studies it acutely increased growth hormone secretion and also raised ACTH and cortisol.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
