Icatibant

Synthetic decapeptide: D-Arg-Arg-Pro-Hyp-Gly-Thi-Ser-D-Tic-Oic-Arg, containing five non-proteinogenic or D-amino acids (hydroxyproline, beta-(2-thienyl)alanine, D-tetrahydroisoquinoline-3-carboxylic acid, octahydroindole-2-carboxylic acid); no standard one-letter sequence is given.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Icatibant acetate · Codes: HOE 140 · Brands: Firazyr

A synthetic ten-residue peptide that blocks the bradykinin B2 receptor. It is an approved prescription drug, given as a single-use prefilled syringe, for treating acute attacks of hereditary angioedema caused by C1-esterase-inhibitor deficiency. Generic versions are approved in the United States. It has also been studied, without approval, for angioedema caused by ACE-inhibitor drugs.

02What the human evidence shows

E1

Acute attacks of hereditary angioedema (C1-inhibitor deficiency)

3published human RCTs
3published human trials
17registered studies
10with posted results

Three randomised, double-blind phase 3 trials were run. FAST-1 (placebo comparator) did not meet its primary endpoint; FAST-2 (tranexamic acid comparator) did; both were reported together. FAST-3, a larger placebo-controlled trial, reported shorter time to symptom relief with icatibant. Results are consistent in direction but FAST-1 was negative on its primary endpoint, which is stated here rather than omitted.

  • [1] Report of FAST-1 (versus placebo, primary endpoint not met) and FAST-2 (versus tranexamic acid, shorter time to symptom relief with icatibant) in acute hereditary angioedema attacks
  • [2] Shorter time to 50% symptom reduction with icatibant than placebo in acute cutaneous and abdominal hereditary angioedema attacks

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “icatibant hereditary angioedema”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E3

ACE-inhibitor-induced angioedema (off-label)

2published human RCTs
2published human trials
4registered studies
2with posted results

Results conflict. A small randomised phase 2 trial (27 participants) compared icatibant with glucocorticoid plus antihistamine and reported faster resolution of swelling. A larger randomised, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in emergency departments found no difference from placebo in time to meeting discharge criteria. The indication is not approved.

  • [3] Shorter time to complete resolution of oedema with icatibant than with glucocorticoid plus antihistamine in a small randomised trial
  • [4] No difference between icatibant and placebo in time to meeting discharge criteria in ACE-inhibitor-induced upper airway angioedema

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “icatibant ACE inhibitor angioedema”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S1Large long-term human exposure (post-marketing surveillance)
  • Injection-site reactions [2]
    Local injection-site reactions (erythema, swelling, pain) were the most commonly reported adverse events in the placebo-controlled FAST-3 trial.

04What is unknown

  • Whether icatibant shortens ACE-inhibitor-induced angioedema is unresolved: a small positive phase 2 trial and a larger negative phase 3 trial conflict
  • No randomised evidence is cited for use as short-term prophylaxis before procedures
  • Efficacy in non-hereditary forms of bradykinin-mediated angioedema other than ACE-inhibitor-induced (e.g. acquired C1-inhibitor deficiency) rests on case reports not reviewed here
  • Status in the UK, Australia, Japan and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Icatibant is a selective competitive antagonist of the bradykinin B2 receptor; in hereditary angioedema, swelling is attributed to excess bradykinin acting at this receptor.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
