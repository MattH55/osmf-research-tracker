Hexarelin

Synthetic hexapeptide His-D-2-methyl-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH2, an analogue of GHRP-6 with a methylated D-tryptophan; contains D-amino acids and a C-terminal amide, so no standard one-letter sequence is given.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Examorelin

A synthetic six-amino-acid growth hormone secretagogue, a close analogue of GHRP-6, that acts on the ghrelin receptor to release growth hormone. It was studied in human endocrine experiments and small clinical studies in the 1990s, including in children with short stature and in adults over 16 weeks, but was never approved as a medicine in any jurisdiction checked. WADA lists it under the name examorelin. It is sold online as a research chemical with claims of raising growth hormone and muscle mass.

02What the human evidence shows

E3

Growth hormone release

n/cpublished human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials
0registered studies
0with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Numerous small human pharmacology studies, many randomised and placebo-controlled with crossover designs, show that hexarelin acutely raises blood growth hormone after intravenous, subcutaneous, intranasal or oral administration, alongside rises in prolactin, ACTH and cortisol. In adults given hexarelin twice daily for 16 weeks the growth hormone response was partly and reversibly attenuated, and IGF-I did not change significantly. The number of such studies was not counted, so both counts are left blank.

  • [1] Acute growth hormone release in humans after intravenous, subcutaneous, intranasal and oral administration
  • [2] Dose-dependent growth hormone, prolactin and cortisol release after intravenous hexarelin in healthy adult men
  • [3] Partial, reversible attenuation of the growth hormone response over 16 weeks in adults, with no significant change in IGF-I

The only adult study identified that measured body composition was an uncontrolled 16-week study: total body fat, lean body mass and bone mineral density did not change significantly from baseline. No randomised trial of muscle mass, strength or body composition was identified.

Short stature in children

An uncontrolled study gave intranasal hexarelin to 8 prepubertal children with short stature for up to 8 months and reported higher IGF-I and faster growth velocity than before treatment. There was no control group, and no adult-height outcome was reported. Hexarelin did not proceed to approval for this use.

  • [4] IGF-I and growth velocity rose from baseline in 8 short prepubertal children in an uncontrolled study

03Safety signals

S3Limited human safety data
  • Acute rise in prolactin and cortisol [2]
    Intravenous hexarelin raised serum prolactin and cortisol in a dose-dependent manner in healthy adult men.
  • Change in pituitary-adrenal response with repeated use [5]
    After 16 weeks of twice-daily subcutaneous hexarelin in adults the cortisol response to an injection was reduced, returning to baseline 4 weeks after stopping.

04What is unknown

  • No randomised trial of muscle mass, strength or body composition was identified; the one uncontrolled adult study found no significant change
  • Human exposure beyond about 8 months was not identified; long-term effects of repeated prolactin and cortisol rises are unstudied
  • Cardiovascular effects in humans are described only in short studies; effects of long-term use on the heart are unknown
  • Australian status was not verified from the TGA register; hexarelin is named in Schedule 4 and Appendix D of the Poisons Standard (October 2026, F2026L01327), which controls supply but is not a product approval
  • Status in the UK, Japan and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Hexarelin is an agonist of the growth hormone secretagogue receptor (the ghrelin receptor); in human studies it acutely increased growth hormone, prolactin, ACTH and cortisol secretion.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
