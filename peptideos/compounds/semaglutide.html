Semaglutide

Acylated analogue of human GLP-1 containing a non-coded amino acid (Aib) and a fatty diacid side chain attached via a linker; not representable as a plain one-letter sequence.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Codes: NN9535 · Brands: Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus, Kayshild · CAS 910463-68-2

A modified version of the gut hormone GLP-1, engineered to persist far longer in the body than the native hormone. It is an approved prescription medicine for type 2 diabetes (Ozempic, and Rybelsus as a tablet) and for chronic weight management (Wegovy), with later approvals in some jurisdictions for cardiovascular risk reduction (UK) and MASH (EU, as Kayshild). Falsified and unlicensed products have been reported by the UK MHRA. Status in China was not verified for this record.

02What the human evidence shows

E1

Type 2 diabetes: glycaemic control and cardiovascular outcomes

n/cpublished human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials
266registered studies
60with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Supported by a large phase 3 programme. In SUSTAIN-6 (3,297 people with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk), major adverse cardiovascular events occurred in 6.6% on semaglutide versus 8.9% on placebo (hazard ratio 0.74). In FLOW (3,533 people with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease), the composite kidney and cardiovascular outcome was 24% lower with semaglutide (hazard ratio 0.76); the trial stopped early for efficacy.

  • [1] Primary cardiovascular outcome in 6.6% vs 8.9% with placebo (HR 0.74) in people with type 2 diabetes
  • [2] Composite kidney/cardiovascular outcome reduced (HR 0.76) in type 2 diabetes with chronic kidney disease

Multiple large placebo-controlled trials (the STEP programme). In STEP 1 (1,961 adults without diabetes), mean body-weight change at week 68 was -14.9% with semaglutide versus -2.4% with placebo. In SELECT (17,604 adults with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease, no diabetes), major cardiovascular events occurred in 6.5% versus 8.0% (hazard ratio 0.80). Discontinuation for adverse events was about twice as common with semaglutide (16.6% vs 8.2%).

  • [3] Mean weight change -14.9% vs -2.4% with placebo at week 68 in adults with overweight or obesity
  • [4] Major adverse cardiovascular events 6.5% vs 8.0% (HR 0.80) in adults with cardiovascular disease and overweight/obesity

03Safety signals

S1Large long-term human exposure (post-marketing surveillance)
  • Gastrointestinal adverse events, including pancreatitis, bowel obstruction and gastroparesis [10]
    Nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea are the most common adverse events in all trials. A US claims-database cohort found higher risk of pancreatitis, bowel obstruction and gastroparesis (not biliary disease) with GLP-1 receptor agonists used for weight loss than with bupropion-naltrexone; confidence intervals were wide. The UK MHRA strengthened pancreatitis warnings for the class in January 2026.
  • Gallbladder and biliary disease [11]
    Meta-analysis of 76 randomised trials (103,371 patients): GLP-1 receptor agonist use associated with increased risk of gallbladder or biliary disease (RR 1.37), higher in weight-loss trials (RR 2.29) and at higher doses.
  • Non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy (NAION) [12]
    Retrospective matched cohort of neuro-ophthalmology patients at one academic institution reported higher NAION risk with semaglutide (HR 4.28 in type 2 diabetes). The MHRA issued a Drug Safety Update on NAION risk for Wegovy, Ozempic and Rybelsus (February 2026).
  • Diabetic retinopathy complications in type 2 diabetes [1]
    In SUSTAIN-6, retinopathy complications (vitreous haemorrhage, blindness, or conditions requiring treatment) were more frequent with semaglutide than placebo.
  • Thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents [13]
    GLP-1 receptor agonists activate thyroid C-cells and cause C-cell proliferation in rodents; relevance to humans is not established.

04What is unknown

  • Long-term effects beyond roughly four years of continuous exposure; the longest outcome trial (SELECT) had mean follow-up of about 40 months
  • Whether the NAION association reported in observational data is causal, and its absolute risk in the general treated population
  • Human relevance of the thyroid C-cell tumours seen in rodents; no adequately sized long-term human study has resolved it
  • Effects of discontinuation on weight and cardiometabolic outcomes over years after stopping
  • Identity, purity and content of compounded and unlicensed semaglutide products, which have not been systematically assayed in the published literature

07Mechanism

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Its fatty-acid side chain binds albumin, which slows clearance and prolongs its duration of action.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
