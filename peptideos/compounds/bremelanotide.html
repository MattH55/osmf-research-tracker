Class A · Approved

Bremelanotide

Cyclic heptapeptide: Ac-Nle-cyclo-(Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys-OH), per the US prescribing information. Contains norleucine and a D-amino acid, so no plain one-letter sequence is given. It differs from melanotan II only at the C-terminus (free acid rather than amide).

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Codes: PT-141 · Brands: Vyleesi

A synthetic cyclic peptide that activates melanocortin receptors. PT-141 is the development code for the same molecule. In the US it is an approved prescription medicine (Vyleesi) for one indication: acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder in premenopausal women. No approval was found in the EU, UK, Canada or Australia. Outside that single US indication, material sold as "PT-141" powder is an unapproved product of unverified identity and purity, regardless of the molecule's approved status in the US.

02What the human evidence shows

E1

Hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in premenopausal women

2published human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials
10registered studies
4with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Two phase 3 randomised placebo-controlled trials (RECONNECT; 1,267 women randomised in total) found small but statistically significant improvements in a sexual-desire score and reductions in desire-related distress compared with placebo. Nausea, flushing and headache were more frequent than with placebo. A 52-week open-label extension (684 entered, 272 completed) reported no new safety signals. An independent re-analysis of the trial data reported substantially higher adverse-event-related discontinuation on bremelanotide and criticised incomplete reporting of protocol-listed outcomes.

  • [1] Two phase 3 RCTs: greater increase in sexual desire score and reduction in related distress than placebo in premenopausal women
  • [2] 52-week open-label extension; nausea (40.4%), flushing and headache were the most common treatment-related adverse events
  • [3] Independent re-analysis based on FDA application data reporting higher adverse-event discontinuation on bremelanotide and incomplete outcome reporting in the phase 3 publication

E3

Erectile dysfunction in men (gray-market claim; not an approved use)

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Small randomised studies from the early 2000s tested an intranasal form in men: a phase 1 study in healthy men and men with mild-to-moderate erectile dysfunction reported erectile responses versus placebo, and a second study reported a larger erectile response when combined with sildenafil. A larger RCT in sildenafil non-responders is subject to a 2023 journal Expression of Concern. The approved US label states the product is not indicated in men or for improving sexual performance. No phase 3 evidence in men exists.

  • [4] Erectile response greater than placebo in a small phase 1 study; flushing and nausea most common adverse events
  • [5] Greater erectile response with combined PT-141 and sildenafil than sildenafil alone in men with erectile dysfunction
  • [6] Reported positive responses in sildenafil non-responders; the article is subject to a 2023 Expression of Concern
  • [7] Journal of Urology Expression of Concern attached to the 2008 RCT

03Safety signals

S2Substantial controlled-trial safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

  • Transient increase in blood pressure and decrease in heart rate after each dose [8]
    US prescribing information Warnings and Precautions 5.1; contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension or known cardiovascular disease. Ambulatory monitoring studied in a dedicated trial.
  • Focal hyperpigmentation (face, gingiva, breasts), not always resolving [9]
    US prescribing information Warnings and Precautions; higher risk with darker skin and with daily use.
  • Nausea, flushing, headache [2]
    Most frequent adverse events in phase 3 trials and the open-label extension.
  • Unknown composition of gray-market PT-141 [9]
    No published analytical testing of gray-market material was identified; safety data from the approved product do not transfer to unverified powders. The approved US product is a single-dose autoinjector solution, not a powder.

04What is unknown

  • No phase 3 or adequately powered trial in men for any indication
  • Efficacy and safety in postmenopausal women are not established
  • Long-term cardiovascular effects of repeated transient blood-pressure rises are not characterised beyond 52 weeks
  • No published analytical data on the identity, purity, sterility or dose accuracy of gray-market PT-141
  • Whether the focal hyperpigmentation seen with frequent use resolves in all cases is not established

07Mechanism

Bremelanotide is a non-selective melanocortin receptor agonist; per its US label, binding at MC1R and MC4R is most relevant at therapeutic exposure, MC1R binding increases pigmentation, and the mechanism in HSDD is unknown.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
