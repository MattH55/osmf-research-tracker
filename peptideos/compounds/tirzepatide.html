Tirzepatide

Synthetic peptide containing non-coded amino acids and a fatty diacid side chain; not representable as a plain one-letter sequence.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Codes: LY3298176 · Brands: Mounjaro, Zepbound · CAS 2023788-19-2

A synthetic peptide that activates the receptors for two gut hormones, GIP and GLP-1. It is an approved prescription medicine for type 2 diabetes (Mounjaro) and for chronic weight management (Zepbound in the US and Japan; Mounjaro carries the weight indication in the UK). The UK MHRA has reported falsified Mounjaro pens in circulation. Status in China was not verified for this record.

02What the human evidence shows

E1

Type 2 diabetes: glycaemic control and cardiovascular outcomes

n/cpublished human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials
70registered studies
28with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Supported by the phase 3 SURPASS programme. In SURPASS-2 (1,879 participants on metformin), all tirzepatide doses lowered HbA1c more than semaglutide (differences -0.15 to -0.45 percentage points). In SURPASS-CVOT (13,299 participants with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease), tirzepatide was non-inferior to dulaglutide for major cardiovascular events (12.2% vs 13.1%, hazard ratio 0.92); superiority was not shown.

  • [1] HbA1c reduction superior to semaglutide at all three tirzepatide doses in type 2 diabetes on metformin
  • [2] Major cardiovascular events 12.2% vs 13.1% with dulaglutide (HR 0.92; non-inferior, not superior)

Multiple large randomised trials (SURMOUNT programme). In SURMOUNT-1 (2,539 adults without diabetes), mean weight change at week 72 ranged from -15.0% to -20.9% across dose groups versus -3.1% with placebo. In SURMOUNT-5 (751 adults without diabetes), weight change at week 72 was -20.2% with tirzepatide versus -13.7% with semaglutide. Gastrointestinal events were the most common adverse events in SURMOUNT-5.

  • [3] Mean weight change -15.0% to -20.9% vs -3.1% with placebo at week 72
  • [4] Weight change -20.2% vs -13.7% with semaglutide at week 72 in adults with obesity without diabetes

One phase 3 trial (SUMMIT, 731 patients, median follow-up 104 weeks). Cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure occurred in 9.9% with tirzepatide versus 15.3% with placebo (hazard ratio 0.62). The difference was driven by worsening heart-failure events; cardiovascular deaths were few (8 vs 5) and did not differ.

03Safety signals

S1Large long-term human exposure (post-marketing surveillance)
  • Gastrointestinal adverse events [2]
    Gastrointestinal events were the most common adverse events in SURMOUNT-5, mostly mild to moderate. SURPASS-CVOT reported more gastrointestinal adverse events with tirzepatide than dulaglutide. The UK MHRA strengthened warnings on acute pancreatitis for GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists in January 2026.
  • Gallbladder and biliary disease (incretin-class signal) [8]
    Meta-analysis of GLP-1 receptor agonist trials found increased gallbladder or biliary disease (RR 1.37); tirzepatide was not part of that analysis, so the class finding is indirect for this compound.
  • Thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents (GLP-1 receptor agonist class) [9]
    GLP-1 receptor agonists cause thyroid C-cell proliferation in rodents; human relevance is not established.

04What is unknown

  • Effect on cardiovascular death and hard outcomes in obesity without diabetes; no placebo-controlled outcome trial in that population has reported in a publication located for this record
  • Whether MASH histology improvements seen in a 190-participant phase 2 trial translate into fewer cirrhosis or liver-related events
  • Safety beyond the roughly two to three years covered by published randomised trials, including long-term gallbladder, pancreatic and thyroid outcomes
  • Separate contribution of GIP receptor agonism versus GLP-1 receptor agonism to the effects seen in humans
  • Content and purity of compounded or unlicensed tirzepatide products; falsified pens have been reported by the MHRA but systematic assay data are lacking

07Mechanism

Tirzepatide is a single peptide that acts as an agonist at both the GIP and GLP-1 receptors. A fatty-acid side chain binds albumin and prolongs its duration of action.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
