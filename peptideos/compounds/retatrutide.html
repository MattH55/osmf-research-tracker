Retatrutide

Synthetic lipidated peptide containing non-coded amino acids; full structure not verified for this record.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Codes: LY3437943

An investigational synthetic peptide from Eli Lilly that activates three hormone receptors: GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon. It is in phase 3 development for obesity, type 2 diabetes and related conditions. It is not approved in any jurisdiction checked. The UK MHRA states it is not authorised and that products sold under the name are sold illegally; case reports describe harm after use of online-purchased material.

02What the human evidence shows

E1

Weight reduction in obesity or overweight (the main use it is sold for)

Two published phase 3 trials and one phase 2 trial. TRIUMPH-1 (2,339 adults without diabetes, 80 weeks): mean weight change -17.6% to -25.0% across dose groups versus -3.9% with placebo. TRIUMPH-2 (1,152 adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes, 80 weeks): -11.9% to -18.8% versus -5.1%. The phase 2 trial (338 adults, 48 weeks) reported -8.7% to -24.2% versus -2.1%. Phase 3 results of TRIUMPH-3 and TRIUMPH-4 (both completed on ClinicalTrials.gov) were not located in a publication.

  • [2] TRIUMPH-2: weight change -11.9% to -18.8% vs -5.1% with placebo at 80 weeks in obesity with type 2 diabetes
  • [3] Phase 2: weight change up to -24.2% vs -2.1% with placebo at 48 weeks

03Safety signals

S2Substantial controlled-trial safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

  • Gastrointestinal adverse events [2]
    In TRIUMPH-2, diarrhoea occurred in 27-34% and nausea in 14-28% of retatrutide groups versus 13% and 8% with placebo. Treatment discontinuation for adverse events or death reached 12% in one dose group versus 5% with placebo.
  • Dysaesthesia (abnormal skin sensation) and hypotension [2]
    In TRIUMPH-2, dysaesthesia occurred in 4-7% versus 1% and hypotension in 1-6% versus <1% with placebo, increasing with dose.
  • Heart rate increase [3]
    In the phase 2 obesity trial, dose-dependent increases in heart rate peaked at 24 weeks and declined thereafter.
  • Harm after use of unlicensed online product [7]
    Case report: a man with type 1 diabetes developed vomiting, diarrhoea, ketonaemia and acute kidney injury after self-administering an online-purchased product marketed as retatrutide (concurrent Shigella infection). Product identity was not confirmed.

04What is unknown

  • Effect on cardiovascular and kidney outcomes; TRIUMPH-Outcomes (NCT06383390) is ongoing
  • Safety beyond 80 weeks of exposure, the longest duration in published randomised trials
  • Mechanism, course and reversibility of the dysaesthesia signal seen in phase 3
  • Results of completed phase 3 trials TRIUMPH-3 and TRIUMPH-4, which were not located in a peer-reviewed publication as of 2026-10-10
  • Identity, purity, sterility and content of material sold online under the retatrutide name; no lawful supply exists and no systematic assay data were located

07Mechanism

Retatrutide is a single peptide with agonist activity at the GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
