KPV

KPV

Tripeptide H-Lys-Pro-Val-OH, corresponding to the C-terminal residues 11-13 of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (alpha-MSH). CAS number as given for KPV (free base) in FDA's July 2026 briefing document; KPV acetate is a distinct salt form. FDA notes that different salts and derivatives are sold under the same name.

Also: Lys-Pro-Val, Lysine-proline-valine, alpha-MSH(11-13) · CAS 67727-97-3

A three-amino-acid peptide (lysine-proline-valine) matching the tail end of the hormone alpha-MSH. It is sold on the gray market for gut inflammation, skin conditions and wound healing. It is not an approved medicine. FDA's 2026 briefing document states that FDA identified no clinical studies and no human exposure data for KPV by any route of administration.

02What the human evidence shows

E6

Inflammatory bowel disease and gut inflammation

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
0published human trials
0registered studies
0with posted results

In mice with chemically induced or immune-transfer colitis, KPV reduced inflammatory markers and tissue damage. Several later studies tested KPV in nanoparticle or hydrogel carriers in rodents. No study in people with Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis or any other gut condition has been published, and FDA found no human data of any kind.

  • [1] Reduced inflammatory cytokine expression in human intestinal cell lines and in mice with DSS- and TNBS-induced colitis
  • [2] Less weight loss and fewer inflammatory infiltrates in two mouse models of colitis

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “KPV”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

Published wound data are limited to animal models; the study cited here used eye drops after corneal scraping in rabbits. No study of skin wounds, surgical wounds or chronic ulcers in humans has been published. FDA reviewed KPV for wound healing in 2026 and noted that none of the references submitted by the nominator studied KPV in humans.

  • [3] Faster corneal epithelial closure after mechanical abrasion in rabbits given topical KPV, blocked by a nitric oxide synthase inhibitor

Evidence is limited to cultured human skin cells and a laboratory three-dimensional skin model, where KPV reduced inflammatory signalling triggered by fine dust particles. No animal model of psoriasis or eczema treated with KPV and no human study were identified.

  • [4] Reduced IL-1beta secretion and cell death in cultured human keratinocytes exposed to fine particulate matter

03Safety signals

S5No human safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

04What is unknown

  • No human pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic data by any route; FDA identified none
  • No human safety data of any kind: FDA found no clinical studies, no FAERS reports and no published case reports
  • Whether KPV taken by mouth survives digestion and reaches inflamed colon tissue in humans is untested; mouse studies relied on drinking water or specialised carriers
  • Which salt or derivative is present in gray-market products is unverified; FDA notes that multiple salts and derivatives, including different active moieties, are sold under the name KPV
  • Immunogenicity in humans has not been assessed
  • Regulatory status outside the US was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

In cell studies KPV is taken up by the PepT1 peptide transporter and reduces NF-kappaB and MAP kinase inflammatory signalling; in mice its effects on colitis appeared to be at least partly independent of the melanocortin-1 receptor.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
