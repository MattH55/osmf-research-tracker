Class C · Gray market

Melanotan II

Lactam-bridged cyclic heptapeptide analogue of alpha-MSH(4-10): Ac-Nle-cyclo[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-NH2, as described by Dorr et al. 1996. Contains norleucine and a D-amino acid. It differs from bremelanotide only at the C-terminus (amide rather than free acid).

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Melanotan-II, Melanotan 2 · Codes: MT-II

A synthetic cyclic peptide related to alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone that activates several melanocortin receptors. It was studied in small academic trials in the 1990s for tanning and erectile function, then not developed as a medicine. It is not approved anywhere checked and is sold online as an unlicensed injectable "tanning" product. Its documented human record is dominated by case reports of harm: changing and new moles, melanoma, priapism, rhabdomyolysis and kidney injury.

02What the human evidence shows

E3

Erectile dysfunction and sexual desire in men

2published human RCTs
3published human trials
1registered studies
0with posted results

Small double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover studies from a single US research group in the late 1990s reported erections after injection more often than after placebo, in men with psychogenic and with organic erectile dysfunction, and higher self-rated sexual desire. In the organic study, 12 of 19 injections initiated erections versus 1 of 21 placebo doses, and 4 of 19 injections caused severe nausea. No larger trial has been published.

  • [1] More erections and higher sexual desire than placebo in a small crossover study; severe nausea after 4 of 19 injections

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “melanotan”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E4

Skin tanning (the main gray-market claim)

No randomised controlled trial in humans has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
1published human trials
1registered studies
0with posted results

The only published efficacy data are a pilot phase 1 study in three healthy men (single-blind, alternating days of injection and saline) in which two subjects showed increased facial and body pigmentation. No randomised trial of tanning has been published. No study has tested whether induced tanning reduces sunburn or skin cancer, and case reports describe new and changing moles in users.

  • [2] Increased skin pigmentation in two of three healthy men; nausea, yawning-stretching and spontaneous erections reported

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “melanotan”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S4Minimal human exposure data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

  • Eruptive and changing melanocytic naevi (moles) [4]
    Case reports of new and changing naevi after unlicensed injection; a review highlights melanocytic changes in existing moles and newly emerging (dysplastic) naevi.
  • Melanoma [5]
    Case reports of melanoma and melanoma in situ arising during or shortly after use; a review counted four reported melanomas. Causation is not established, and users often also use sunbeds.
  • Melanoma in situ [6]
    Australian case report of melanotan-associated melanoma in situ.
  • Ischaemic priapism [7]
    Case report of acute ischaemic priapism requiring surgical decompression after injection; the authors note earlier reports.
  • Systemic sympathomimetic toxicity and rhabdomyolysis with kidney injury [8]
    Case report after injection of an internet-purchased product.
  • Renal infarction [9]
    Case report and literature review attributing renal infarction most likely to melanotan II.
  • Overall risk profile of unregulated use [10]
    Review of unregulated alpha-MSH analogue use summarising cutaneous complications and uncertainties about preparation and contents.

04What is unknown

  • No randomised trial of tanning efficacy, and no study of whether induced pigmentation changes sunburn or skin-cancer risk
  • Incidence of naevus changes, melanoma, priapism or kidney injury among users is unknown; only case reports exist
  • No human pharmacokinetic data for the injectable or nasal products sold online
  • No published analytical data on identity, purity, sterility or content of gray-market products
  • No long-term human safety data of any kind
  • Results of the only registered current trial (NCT07437560, vitiligo, small company sponsor, recruiting) are not available

07Mechanism

Melanotan II is a non-selective agonist at several melanocortin receptors; MC1 receptor activation on melanocytes increases melanin production, and central melanocortin receptor activation was associated with erections and reduced feeding in early human and rodent studies.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
