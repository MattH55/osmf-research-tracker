Sermorelin

YADAIFTNSYRKVLGQLSARKLLQDIMSR

Residues 1-29 of human growth hormone-releasing hormone with a C-terminal amide.

Also: Sermorelin acetate, GHRH(1-29)NH2, GRF(1-29)

A synthetic copy of the first 29 amino acids of human growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH). In the US it was approved as Geref, for testing pituitary growth hormone release (1990) and for idiopathic growth hormone deficiency in children (1997). The manufacturer discontinued Geref in 2008 and FDA withdrew both approvals in 2009; FDA later determined the withdrawal was not for safety or effectiveness reasons. No sermorelin product is currently approved in the US. It is offered by compounders and "anti-ageing" clinics for adult uses never tested in an approval trial.

02What the human evidence shows

E4

Idiopathic growth hormone deficiency in children (former US-approved use)

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

The principal published efficacy study was an open-label, uncontrolled multicentre study in 110 previously untreated prepubertal children with GH deficiency; mean height velocity rose over 12 months, and 74% were classed as good responders at 6 months. No randomised controlled trial against placebo or growth hormone was identified in this review. The product has not been marketed in the US since 2008.

  • [1] Mean height velocity increased over 12 months in an open-label study of 110 prepubertal GH-deficient children

E4

Age-related decline in growth hormone, body composition and strength in older adults (anti-ageing claims)

No randomised controlled trial in humans has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
2published human trials
27registered studies
11with posted results

Evidence is limited to very small, short studies in healthy older men, without a placebo group. In 10 older men, two weeks of GHRH(1-29) raised GH and IGF-1 to levels seen in young men (dose order randomised, no placebo). In 11 older men over six weeks, GH rose but IGF-1, body weight, muscle and fat by DEXA did not change; two of six strength measures improved. No trial has tested body composition, function, frailty or any clinical outcome against placebo. Trials of tesamorelin, a different GHRH analogue, are not evidence for sermorelin.

  • [2] Raised 24-hour GH and IGF-1 over 14 days in 10 healthy older men
  • [3] Raised nocturnal GH without change in IGF-1, lean or fat mass over 6 weeks in 11 older men; 2 of 6 strength measures improved

03Safety signals

S3Limited human safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

04What is unknown

  • No placebo-controlled trial of sermorelin in adults for body composition, strength, sleep, cognition or any other outcome claimed by anti-ageing clinics
  • No long-term safety data in adults; the published adult studies lasted two to six weeks
  • Quality and identity of compounded sermorelin are not covered by any current FDA approval
  • Whether stimulating GH and IGF-1 over long periods in older adults alters cancer or metabolic risk has not been studied for sermorelin
  • Post-marketing safety data from the Geref era are not published in a form that allows exposure-adjusted rates; FDA's 2013 notice reported only that its review found no safety reason for withdrawal
  • EU, UK, Australian, Japanese and Chinese status (including any historic national authorisations) was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Sermorelin binds pituitary GHRH receptors; in human studies it acutely and repeatedly increased growth hormone secretion.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
