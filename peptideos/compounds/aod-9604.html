AOD-9604

Described by its developers as Tyr-hGH(177-191): a tyrosine residue followed by residues 177-191 of human growth hormone. The exact residue sequence and any disulfide bond were not verified from a primary source for this record, so no one-letter sequence is given.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Tyr-hGH(177-191) · Codes: AOD9604

A synthetic peptide fragment from the end of human growth hormone, developed by the Australian company Metabolic Pharmaceuticals as an oral anti-obesity drug. Six placebo-controlled trials were run in the early 2000s; development stopped in 2007 after a large 24-week trial reportedly found no significant weight loss versus placebo. It is not an approved medicine, and is sold on the gray market with fat-loss and joint claims.

02What the human evidence shows

E3

Weight loss and fat reduction in obesity

n/cpublished human trials

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

The developer ran six randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in about 900 adults, mostly with obesity, by mouth or single intravenous doses. Their efficacy results were never published as primary peer-reviewed reports; the published summary covers safety only. A review states that a 12-week trial showed modestly greater weight loss than placebo, but that development ended after a 24-week trial in 536 people failed to show significant weight loss. The evidence is therefore conflicting and incompletely reported. No trial of injected AOD-9604 for weight loss was identified. Rodent studies reported reduced weight gain and increased fat breakdown.

  • [1] Sponsor-funded safety summary of six randomised placebo-controlled trials; reports safety outcomes, not weight-loss results
  • [2] Review reporting that development stopped in 2007 after a 24-week trial in 536 participants did not show significant weight loss
  • [3] Reduced body weight gain and increased fat oxidation in obese mice, without the blood-glucose effects of full growth hormone

03Safety signals

S2Substantial controlled-trial safety data
  • Serious adverse events of unclear causality (FDA) [5]
    FDA states that it has identified serious adverse events that may be associated with AOD-9604, though causality is not clear, and that it has no, or only limited, safety information. The sponsor-funded trial summary reports no AOD-9604-related withdrawals or serious adverse events and more gastrointestinal events at the highest oral dose.

04What is unknown

  • Full weight-loss results of the six developer trials, including the failed 24-week trial, have never been published in a peer-reviewed journal
  • The human trials used oral or single intravenous doses; no published human data cover repeated subcutaneous injection
  • No trial lasted longer than 24 weeks, so long-term human safety is unknown
  • FDA's reported serious adverse events have not been described publicly in enough detail to judge causality
  • Identity and purity of gray-market products are unverified; a 2014 Belgian analysis of seized products was a case report only
  • Regulatory status outside the US was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

AOD-9604 does not bind the growth hormone receptor in cell assays. In obese mice it increased fat oxidation and markers of fat breakdown without the blood-glucose effects of full growth hormone; its molecular target is not established.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
