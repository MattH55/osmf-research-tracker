Class C · Gray market

Follistatin-344

An alternatively spliced isoform of human follistatin, a cysteine-rich secreted glycoprotein rather than a short peptide. Black-market products analysed in 2019 contained a His-tagged recombinant protein with a high proportion of oligomers.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Follistatin 344, FS344, FST344

A form of follistatin, a natural protein that binds and blocks myostatin and related growth factors that limit muscle growth. Gray-market vials labelled "follistatin 344" are sold for muscle building. That is different from the clinical research, which used a gene-therapy virus carrying the follistatin-344 gene, injected into the thigh muscles of a few patients with muscular dystrophy or inclusion body myositis. No follistatin product is approved as a medicine in any jurisdiction checked.

02What the human evidence shows

E4

Muscle disease via follistatin-344 gene therapy (a different intervention)

No randomised controlled trial in humans has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
2published human trials
15registered studies
1with posted results

Gene therapy is not the gray-market product. An adeno-associated virus carrying the follistatin-344 gene was injected into the thigh muscles of six men with Becker muscular dystrophy and, separately, six people with sporadic inclusion body myositis. Walking distance improved in four of six in each study, with no adverse effects reported; the myositis study compared against eight untreated patients, not a randomised control. In macaques, the same vector increased muscle size and strength.

  • [1] Uncontrolled trial in six patients with Becker muscular dystrophy; four improved on the six-minute walk test
  • [2] Six treated patients with inclusion body myositis compared with eight untreated matched patients on six-minute walk distance
  • [3] Increased quadriceps size and strength in cynomolgus macaques after intramuscular AAV1-FS344 gene transfer

E8

Muscle growth and strength (injected follistatin-344 protein)

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
0published human trials
15registered studies
1with posted results

No published study of injected follistatin-344 protein in any model, animal or human, was identified. Animal studies of follistatin protein used other forms: an engineered follistatin-315 fusion protein increased lean mass in mice, while follistatin-288 protein in rats had mixed effects, including smaller muscles and less force in some groups. These results cannot be transferred to gray-market follistatin-344, whose stability, activity and content are unknown.

  • [4] An engineered follistatin-315 Fc fusion protein (not follistatin-344) increased lean muscle mass in mice
  • [5] Follistatin-288 protein (not follistatin-344) had mixed effects on muscle weight and force in rats after nerve injury

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “follistatin”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S5No human safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

  • Mislabelled and uncharacterised black-market products [6]
    In an anti-doping laboratory analysis of 17 black-market products sold as follistatin, only nine contained follistatin; some others contained different growth-promoting peptides. All nine follistatin-containing products held a His-tagged recombinant protein with a high degree of oligomers.

04What is unknown

  • No human or animal study of injected follistatin-344 protein was identified, for efficacy, pharmacokinetics or safety
  • Whether injected recombinant follistatin-344 protein stays active long enough in the body to affect muscle is unknown
  • Follistatin also binds activins, which act in reproductive and other tissues; effects of systemic exposure in humans are unstudied
  • Immunogenicity of His-tagged, partly oligomerised black-market protein has not been assessed
  • The gene-therapy findings come from 12 patients without randomised controls and do not apply to protein injections
  • Regulatory status outside the US was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Follistatin binds myostatin and activins and blocks their signalling through activin type II receptors; in mice and macaques, raising follistatin levels by gene transfer increased skeletal muscle size.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
