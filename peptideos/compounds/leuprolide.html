Leuprorelin

Synthetic GnRH agonist nonapeptide: pGlu-His-Trp-Ser-Tyr-D-Leu-Leu-Arg-Pro-NHEt (N-terminal pyroglutamate, D-leucine at position 6, C-terminal ethylamide); no standard one-letter sequence is given.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Leuprolide, Leuprolide acetate, Leuprorelin mesilate · Brands: Lupron Depot, Lupron, Camcevi

A synthetic nine-residue analogue of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH). After an initial surge, continued exposure suppresses pituitary gonadotropin release and lowers testosterone or oestrogen to castrate or postmenopausal levels. It is an approved prescription drug in long-acting injectable forms. Labelled uses include advanced prostate cancer, endometriosis, uterine fibroids (with iron, before surgery) and central precocious puberty, varying by product and jurisdiction.

02What the human evidence shows

E2

Advanced prostate cancer (androgen deprivation)

1published human RCTs
1published human trials
272registered studies
110with posted results

A randomised trial in metastatic prostate cancer compared leuprolide with diethylstilbestrol, the hormonal standard of the time, and reported similar disease response with fewer cardiovascular and breast adverse effects. The wider GnRH-agonist trial literature is larger than the single RCT cited here; only the verified trial is counted.

  • [1] Comparable response rates with leuprolide and diethylstilbestrol in men with metastatic prostate cancer, with fewer cardiovascular and breast adverse effects in the leuprolide arm

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “leuprolide prostate cancer”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E2

Endometriosis (pain)

2published human RCTs
2published human trials
31registered studies
8with posted results

A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial reported reduced endometriosis-associated pain with depot leuprolide. A 12-month randomised trial tested hormonal add-back regimens alongside leuprolide to limit bone loss and menopausal symptoms. Hypo-oestrogenic adverse effects, including bone mineral density loss, limit duration of use.

  • [2] Greater reduction in endometriosis symptoms with depot leuprolide than with placebo in a randomised double-blind trial
  • [3] Randomised comparison of hormonal add-back regimens given with depot leuprolide in women with endometriosis, assessing pain relief and bone mineral density

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “leuprolide endometriosis”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E4

Central precocious puberty

No randomised controlled trial in humans has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
1published human trials
23registered studies
10with posted results

Approval and current evidence in central precocious puberty rest on single-arm, open-label studies measuring suppression of the pituitary-gonadal axis, such as a recent phase 3 study of a long-acting formulation. A randomised placebo-controlled design is not used in this condition; no RCT is cited.

  • [5] Single-arm phase 3 study reporting suppression of luteinising hormone in children with central precocious puberty

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “leuprolide precocious puberty”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S1Large long-term human exposure (post-marketing surveillance)
  • Diabetes and cardiovascular disease during androgen deprivation [6]
    In a large observational cohort of men with prostate cancer, GnRH-agonist therapy was associated with higher risk of incident diabetes, coronary heart disease, myocardial infarction and sudden cardiac death.
  • Bone mineral density loss with hypo-oestrogenism [3]
    Bone loss during leuprolide treatment for endometriosis was the reason hormonal add-back regimens were tested in a randomised trial.

04What is unknown

  • The single prostate cancer RCT cited predates modern trial registration; registered-trial denominators for leuprolide will undercount the pre-2000 evidence base
  • Long-term outcomes of childhood treatment for central precocious puberty (adult height, fertility, bone health) are not established by the single-arm study cited
  • Whether the cardiometabolic associations seen in observational androgen-deprivation cohorts are causal has not been established by randomised data cited here
  • Status in the UK, Australia, Japan and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Leuprolide is a GnRH receptor agonist; in human studies continuous exposure, after an initial rise, down-regulates pituitary GnRH receptors and suppresses luteinising hormone, follicle-stimulating hormone and sex-steroid levels.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
