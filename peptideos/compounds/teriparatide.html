Teriparatide

SVSEIQLMHNLGKHLNSMERVEWLRKKLQDVHNF

Identical to the 34 N-terminal residues of the 84-residue human parathyroid hormone; Forteo is produced in a recombinant Escherichia coli strain (US label, section 11).

Also: PTH(1-34), rhPTH(1-34), Parathyroid hormone (1-34) · Brands: Forteo, Forsteo, Bonsity, Movymia, Terrosa, Livogiva, Sondelbay, Kauliv, Teriparatide SUN, Zandoriah, Osnuvo

A 34-amino-acid fragment of human parathyroid hormone, the region responsible for its biological activity. It is an approved prescription medicine for osteoporosis in the US, EU, Canada and Japan, sold originally as Forteo (Forsteo in the EU) and now also as several follow-on and biosimilar products. Unlike most osteoporosis drugs, which slow bone breakdown, it is classed as a bone-forming (anabolic) agent. Regulatory status in the UK, Australia and China was not verified for this record.

02What the human evidence shows

E1

Postmenopausal osteoporosis (fracture prevention)

2published human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials
182registered studies
63with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Two large randomised controlled trials with fracture endpoints support this approved indication. In the pivotal placebo-controlled trial of 1,637 postmenopausal women with prior vertebral fracture, teriparatide reduced new vertebral and nonvertebral fragility fractures and increased bone mineral density. In VERO, a double-blind trial of 1,360 women with severe osteoporosis, new vertebral fractures were less frequent with teriparatide than with the active comparator risedronate. Reduction of hip fracture has not been demonstrated (EMA product overview).

  • [1] Reduced new vertebral and nonvertebral fragility fractures versus placebo in postmenopausal women with prior vertebral fracture
  • [2] New vertebral fractures in 5.4% with teriparatide versus 12.0% with risedronate over 24 months in women with severe osteoporosis

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “teriparatide”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

One randomised placebo-controlled trial in 437 men with low bone density showed increased spine and hip bone mineral density. Its endpoint was bone density, not fracture; the trial was stopped early, after a median of about 11 months, because of the rat osteosarcoma finding. The US label accordingly describes the male indication as increasing bone mass, not reducing fractures.

  • [3] Increased lumbar spine and femoral neck bone mineral density versus placebo in men with osteoporosis

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “teriparatide AND male osteoporosis”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

One randomised double-blind trial of 428 adults on long-term glucocorticoids compared teriparatide with alendronate. Lumbar spine bone mineral density rose more with teriparatide (primary endpoint), and fewer new vertebral fractures were recorded in the teriparatide group (a secondary outcome); serum calcium elevations were more common with teriparatide. No placebo-controlled fracture trial exists in this population.

  • [4] Greater increase in lumbar spine bone mineral density with teriparatide than with alendronate in glucocorticoid-treated adults

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “teriparatide AND glucocorticoid”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

Acceleration of fracture healing (off-label claim)

Not an approved use. In a randomised double-blind trial of 102 postmenopausal women with distal radius fractures, the prespecified primary comparison (higher-dose teriparatide versus placebo) showed no difference in time to radiographic healing; a shorter healing time in the lower-dose arm came from a post hoc analysis that the authors said should be interpreted with caution. No adequately powered trial has shown faster fracture healing.

  • [5] Primary hypothesis (shorter time to cortical bridging with the higher-dose arm versus placebo) not supported in women with distal radial fractures

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “teriparatide AND fracture healing”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S1Large long-term human exposure (post-marketing surveillance)
  • Osteosarcoma in rats [6]
    Long-term treatment of F344 rats produced bone neoplasms, including osteosarcoma, with incidence dependent on dose and treatment duration. The US label retains an osteosarcoma warning and states there are limited data on risk beyond 2 years of use.
  • Osteosarcoma in humans: no increase detected in surveillance [7]
    A 15-year US postmarketing surveillance study found adult osteosarcoma incidence among teriparatide users no different from the expected background rate. The current US label states that osteosarcoma has been reported post-marketing but an increased risk has not been observed in human observational studies; it carries no boxed warning.
  • Hypercalcaemia, hypersensitivity reactions, cutaneous calcification and urolithiasis risk [8]
    Listed in Warnings and Precautions of the current US label (revised 08/2026), which added hypersensitivity reactions as a recent major change.

04What is unknown

  • Osteosarcoma risk with use beyond 2 years: the US label states data are limited
  • Hip fracture reduction has not been demonstrated in randomised trials (EMA product overview)
  • No fracture-endpoint randomised trial in men; the male indication rests on bone density
  • No adequately powered randomised evidence that it speeds healing of acute fractures
  • Teriparatide is not named on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List; whether anti-doping authorities would treat it under a catch-all clause was not assessed
  • Regulatory status in the UK, Australia and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Teriparatide binds the same cell-surface receptors as endogenous parathyroid hormone in bone and kidney; intermittent exposure in clinical trials increased markers of bone formation and bone mineral density.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
