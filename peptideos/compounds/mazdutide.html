Mazdutide

Synthetic lipidated peptide; full structure not verified for this record.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Codes: IBI362, LY3305677 · Brands: Xinermei · CAS 2259884-03-0

A synthetic peptide that activates the glucagon and GLP-1 receptors, co-developed by Innovent Biologics and Eli Lilly. A peer-reviewed drug-development review (Drugs, 2025) reports approval in China in June 2025 for long-term weight management and in September 2025 for type 2 diabetes. It is not approved in the US, EU, Canada, Australia or Japan, where it remains investigational.

02What the human evidence shows

E1

Weight reduction in obesity or overweight

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

GLORY-1 (phase 3, 610 Chinese adults): weight change at week 48 was -11.00% and -14.01% in the two dose groups versus +0.30% with placebo. A US phase 2 trial (179 adults, Lilly-funded) reported -7.3% to -18.1% across dose groups versus -0.9% with placebo at 32 weeks; discontinuation for adverse events reached 20% in the highest dose group.

  • [1] GLORY-1: weight change -11.00% and -14.01% vs +0.30% with placebo at week 48
  • [2] US phase 2: weight change -7.3% to -18.1% vs -0.9% with placebo at 32 weeks

Type 2 diabetes: glycaemic control

1with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Two phase 3 trials in Chinese adults. Versus placebo (320 participants, 24 weeks), HbA1c fell 1.57 and 2.15 percentage points versus 0.14. Versus the approved GLP-1 receptor agonist dulaglutide (731 participants, 28 weeks), both mazdutide doses were non-inferior and superior for HbA1c (differences -0.24 and -0.30 points) with greater weight loss. Gastrointestinal adverse events were more frequent than with dulaglutide.

  • [3] HbA1c change -1.57 and -2.15 vs -0.14 points with placebo at 24 weeks
  • [4] HbA1c superior to dulaglutide (differences -0.24 and -0.30 points) at 28 weeks

03Safety signals

S2Substantial controlled-trial safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

  • Gastrointestinal adverse events [4]
    Diarrhoea, nausea and vomiting were the most common adverse events in phase 3 trials; incidence was higher than with dulaglutide in DREAMS-2. In the US phase 2 trial, discontinuation for adverse events reached 20% in the highest dose group, mainly gastrointestinal.

04What is unknown

  • Cardiovascular outcomes; no cardiovascular outcome trial was located on ClinicalTrials.gov or in publications
  • Efficacy and safety in non-Chinese populations beyond a single 179-participant US phase 2 trial
  • Safety beyond 48 weeks of exposure, the longest duration in the published phase 3 trials
  • Post-marketing safety experience in China since 2025; no surveillance data were located
  • Exact wording and scope of the China approvals as issued by the NMPA, which could not be read for this record

07Mechanism

Mazdutide is a peptide agonist of both the glucagon receptor and the GLP-1 receptor.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
