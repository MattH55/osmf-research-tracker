Afamelanotide

Linear tridecapeptide analogue of alpha-MSH: Ac-Ser-Tyr-Ser-Nle-Glu-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Gly-Lys-Pro-Val-NH2, per the US prescribing information. Contains norleucine and a D-amino acid, so no plain one-letter sequence is given.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Melanotan-1, MT-1, [Nle4, D-Phe7]-alpha-MSH · Codes: CUV1647 · Brands: Scenesse

A synthetic analogue of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone that binds mainly to the melanocortin-1 receptor and increases eumelanin in the skin. It is approved as a controlled-release subcutaneous implant (Scenesse) for adults with erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP), a rare inherited condition causing painful reactions to light, in the US, EU, UK, Canada and Australia. The same molecule (Melanotan-1) is also sold as unapproved powder for cosmetic tanning; that material is not the approved product.

02What the human evidence shows

E1

Phototoxicity in erythropoietic protoporphyria (adults)

2published human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials
23registered studies
12with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Two multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCTs (74 patients in the EU, 94 in the US) found longer pain-free direct sunlight exposure with afamelanotide (US median 69.4 versus 40.8 hours over 6 months; EU median 6.0 versus 0.8 hours over 9 months) and improved quality of life. An observational study of 115 patients treated for up to 8 years at two centres reported sustained use and only minor adverse events. The EU authorisation is under exceptional circumstances because comprehensive data could not be obtained.

  • [1] Two RCTs: longer pain-free sunlight exposure and fewer phototoxic reactions (EU trial) than placebo in EPP
  • [2] Sustained use and improved EPP quality-of-life scores over up to 8 years; mainly minor adverse events such as nausea

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “afamelanotide”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E3

Nonsegmental vitiligo (with narrowband UV-B phototherapy)

1published human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials
23registered studies

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

One small randomised multicentre trial (55 patients, skin phototypes III-VI) found faster and greater repigmentation with afamelanotide plus narrowband UV-B than with UV-B alone. A larger phase 3 trial is registered and active but had not reported on the retrieval date. Vitiligo is not an approved indication in any jurisdiction checked.

  • [3] Greater and earlier repigmentation with combination therapy than narrowband UV-B alone in a 55-patient RCT

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “afamelanotide”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E3

Cosmetic tanning in healthy people (gray-market 'Melanotan-1' claim)

2published human RCTs
3published human trials
23registered studies
12with posted results

Three small phase 1 studies in healthy volunteers (two of them randomising 8 subjects each, one open-label with 12) reported skin darkening and enhanced tanning with sunlight or UV-B. These were safety studies of short duration. There is no evidence that induced tanning reduces skin cancer or sunburn in real-world use, and no data on gray-market material.

  • [4] Enhanced tanning with sunlight or UV-B in small phase 1 studies of healthy volunteers; nausea and transient facial flushing reported

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “afamelanotide”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S2Substantial controlled-trial safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

  • Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis [5]
    US prescribing information Warnings and Precautions 5.1.
  • Darkening of pre-existing naevi and ephelides [5]
    US prescribing information Warnings and Precautions 5.2 (skin monitoring); periodic full-body skin examination is recommended in the label.
  • Nausea [2]
    Most frequently recorded adverse event attributed to afamelanotide in long-term observational use.

04What is unknown

  • Efficacy and safety in children and adolescents with EPP are not established
  • Long-term melanocytic effects (naevus changes, melanoma risk) beyond observational follow-up of about a hundred patients are not characterised
  • No adequately powered RCT in vitiligo has reported
  • No data on identity, purity or sterility of gray-market 'Melanotan-1' powders, or on their safety outside the approved implant formulation

07Mechanism

Afamelanotide is an alpha-MSH analogue that binds predominantly to the melanocortin-1 receptor and increases eumelanin production in the skin independently of UV exposure.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
