Dihexa

N-hexanoic-Tyr-Ile-(6) aminohexanoic amide: a modified dipeptide (Tyr-Ile) with an N-terminal hexanoyl group and a C-terminal 6-aminohexanoic amide; non-standard groups, so no one-letter sequence is given.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Dihexa acetate, N-hexanoic-Tyr-Ile-(6) aminohexanoic amide

A small, chemically modified peptide derived from angiotensin IV, designed at a US university to be stable and to reach the brain when taken by mouth. It is sold online as a research chemical with claims of improving memory and cognition. It has never been tested in people in any published study identified, and no approved medicine contains it. Two journal papers from the developing laboratory on the angiotensin IV/HGF approach were retracted in 2025, and the original dihexa report carries an expression of concern.

02What the human evidence shows

E6

Cognitive enhancement / dementia

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
0published human trials
0registered studies
0with posted results

All efficacy data are from rodents. The developing group reported that dihexa reversed scopolamine-induced and age-related deficits in a water-maze test in rats; the journal attached an expression of concern to that report in 2021. A separate group reported improved spatial learning in APP/PS1 Alzheimer-model mice. A 2014 paper from the developing laboratory proposing that these effects depend on the HGF/c-Met system was retracted in 2025. No human study of any kind was identified.

  • [1] Reversed scopolamine-induced and age-related water-maze deficits in rats (report subject to a 2021 expression of concern)
  • [2] Improved spatial learning in the water maze and reduced glial activation in APP/PS1 Alzheimer-model mice
  • [3] Retraction of the 2014 rodent and cell report linking these peptides' effects to the HGF/c-Met system

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “dihexa”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S5No human safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

04What is unknown

  • No human data of any kind were identified: no pharmacokinetic, safety or efficacy study in people
  • FDA states it has not identified any human exposure data and lacks information on whether dihexa would cause harm in humans
  • The proposed mechanism through hepatocyte growth factor and its receptor c-Met is a pathway also involved in cell growth and cancer; long-term effects of stimulating it in humans are unstudied
  • Independent replication of the original rat findings is limited to one mouse study identified, and the founding reports are under an expression of concern or retracted
  • Status in the UK, Australia, Japan and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Dihexa was derived from angiotensin IV. Its developers proposed that it acts by enhancing hepatocyte growth factor signalling through the c-Met receptor; a key 2014 paper supporting that mechanism has been retracted, so the mechanism is not established.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
