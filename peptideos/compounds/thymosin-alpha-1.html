Thymalfasin

SDAAVDTSSEITTKDLKEKKEVVEEAEN

28 residues with an acetylated N-terminal serine; corresponds to residues 2-29 of human prothymosin alpha (UniProt P06454).

Also: Thymosin alpha-1, Thymosin α1, Ta1, Tα1 · Brands: Zadaxin

A 28-amino-acid peptide originally isolated from thymus tissue, made synthetically as thymalfasin (brand Zadaxin). It is an approved prescription medicine in some countries, mainly for chronic hepatitis B and as an immune adjunct, but is not approved in the United States. FDA states it is not approved in the US, Japan or Europe except Italy. In the US it is sold through compounding and gray-market channels with claims for viral infections, chronic fatigue, Lyme disease, cancer and general immune support.

02What the human evidence shows

E3

Chronic hepatitis B

n/cpublished human trials
6registered studies
0with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Several small randomised trials, mostly from the 1990s, gave mixed results. A Taiwanese trial of 98 patients reported higher combined viral clearance at 18 months with a 26-week course than with no treatment. A US phase 3 double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 97 patients did not show a statistically significant difference. A meta-analysis of four small trials against interferon alpha found responses accumulated after treatment ended. FDA's 2024 review judged the evidence insufficient, noting outdated comparators and assays; none of the trials tested current standard antivirals as comparators.

  • [1] Higher complete virological response at 18 months than untreated controls in 98 patients with chronic hepatitis B
  • [2] Complete response 14% versus 4% with placebo in 97 patients, not statistically significant; authors state results do not confirm earlier reports
  • [3] Across four small randomised trials (199 patients), responses were not better than interferon alpha at end of treatment but higher six months after treatment

Chronic hepatitis C

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 552 patients who had not responded to earlier interferon-based therapy found no difference in sustained viral response when thymosin alpha-1 was added to peginterferon and ribavirin (12.7% versus 10.5%); a higher rate appeared only in a subgroup completing all 48 weeks. A small earlier placebo-controlled pilot reported no viral clearance. These trials used older interferon regimens, not current direct-acting antivirals.

  • [4] No difference in sustained viral response versus placebo in the intention-to-treat population of 552 patients

Sepsis

2published human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials
6registered studies
0with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

The largest trial, TESTS (1,106 adults with sepsis in 22 Chinese centres, double-blind, placebo-controlled), found no difference in 28-day mortality (23.4% versus 24.1%). An earlier single-blind randomised trial (ETASS, 361 patients) reported lower 28-day mortality with a marginal p-value. The randomised evidence is therefore conflicting, and the larger, better-blinded trial was negative. All trials were conducted in China.

  • [5] No difference in 28-day all-cause mortality versus placebo in 1,089 analysed adults with sepsis
  • [6] 28-day mortality 26.0% versus 35.0% in 361 patients with severe sepsis, marginal statistical significance

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “thymosin alpha 1 sepsis”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

Cancer (adjunct to chemotherapy; metastatic melanoma as the main tested setting)

1published human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials
26registered studies
0with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

A randomised trial in 488 patients with metastatic melanoma compared dacarbazine plus interferon alpha with arms adding thymosin alpha-1. More tumour responses were seen in two thymosin arms, but the differences in overall and progression-free survival did not reach statistical significance. FDA's 2024 review of liver and lung cancer studies found small randomised trials, single-arm and retrospective studies, and concluded the evidence was insufficient. These regimens are not current standard care.

  • [7] More tumour responses with thymosin alpha-1 arms in 488 patients with metastatic melanoma; survival differences not statistically significant

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “thymosin alpha 1 cancer”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

Evidence comes mainly from retrospective cohort studies early in the pandemic. A meta-analysis pooling eight studies reported lower mortality with marked heterogeneity and no difference in need for mechanical ventilation or length of stay; its authors called for randomised trials. FDA's 2024 review noted that three of four meta-analyses found no mortality reduction and judged the information insufficient.

  • [8] Pooled data from eight mostly non-randomised studies associated treatment with lower mortality (high heterogeneity), with no difference in ventilation need or hospital stay

04What is unknown

  • Approval status in China, Italy and other countries where it is reportedly marketed was not verified from those regulators' own sources; FDA states it cannot independently verify the claimed approvals in all countries
  • Immunogenicity of the 28-residue peptide is not characterised; FDA notes possible aggregation at higher concentrations
  • Safety in haematopoietic stem cell transplant recipients is uncertain; FDA raised concern about graft-versus-host disease or engraftment failure
  • No randomised trial compares thymosin alpha-1 with current standard antivirals for hepatitis B or C
  • Claims for Lyme disease, allergies, asthma, psoriatic arthritis and general immune support were not tiered here; no controlled human study for them was identified in this review
  • Not named on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List; S0 covers only substances with no current approval anywhere, and FDA reports approval in Italy, but WADA's own classification of this peptide was not confirmed

07Mechanism

Thymosin alpha-1 is an immunomodulatory peptide whose effects are attributed in part to interaction with Toll-like receptor 9 on dendritic and lymphoid progenitor cells.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
