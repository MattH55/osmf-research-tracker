Class C · Gray market

Selank

TKPRPGP

Heptapeptide: the immune peptide tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg) extended with Pro-Gly-Pro at the C-terminus for stability.

Also: Selank acetate, Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro · Codes: TP-7

A synthetic seven-amino-acid peptide made by adding a Pro-Gly-Pro tail to tuftsin, a short peptide derived from an antibody fragment. It was developed in Russia as an anti-anxiety agent, and its clinical literature is almost entirely Russian. It is sold online elsewhere as a research chemical with claims for anxiety, focus and memory. No approved medicine contains it in any jurisdiction checked for this record.

02What the human evidence shows

E3

Anxiety disorders

n/cpublished human RCTs
3published human trials
2registered studies
0with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Three small Russian clinical studies were identified, none placebo-controlled. In 62 patients with generalised anxiety disorder or neurasthenia, selank and the benzodiazepine medazepam had similar anxiolytic effects. In 60 patients with anxiety and somatoform disorders, selank was compared with phenazepam. In 70 patients, adding selank to phenazepam was reported to bring an earlier response and fewer benzodiazepine side effects. PubMed indexes two of these as randomised; the designs were not verified from full texts, so the RCT count is left blank. Without placebo arms these studies cannot separate drug effect from the high placebo response typical of anxiety trials.

  • [1] Similar anxiolytic effect of selank and medazepam in 62 patients with generalised anxiety disorder or neurasthenia
  • [2] Anxiolytic effect reported with selank in a comparison with phenazepam in 60 patients with anxiety and somatoform disorders
  • [3] Earlier response and fewer phenazepam side effects reported when selank was added to phenazepam in 70 patients with anxiety disorders

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “selank”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

Memory findings are from rodents: in rats given ethanol long term, selank prevented memory and attention impairment in an object-recognition test and prevented an ethanol-induced rise in hippocampal BDNF. No human trial with cognitive performance as an outcome in healthy people was identified; one anxiety trial above used attention tests as secondary measures in patients also taking phenazepam.

  • [4] Prevented ethanol-induced memory and attention impairment in an object-recognition test in rats

One small rat study of experimental intracerebral haemorrhage (five animals per group) reported better functional recovery with selank without an effect on haematoma volume or swelling. No human study of selank in stroke was identified.

  • [5] Improved functional recovery without change in haematoma volume in a rat model of intracerebral haemorrhage

03Safety signals

S4Minimal human exposure data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

04What is unknown

  • Sport status is not given: selank is not named on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List, and whether class S0 applies depends on whether any governmental health authority has approved it, which this record did not verify from a regulator source outside the tracked jurisdictions
  • No placebo-controlled trial of selank in any anxiety disorder was identified
  • No human trial of cognitive performance in healthy people was identified
  • No systematic human safety data were identified; FDA states it lacks important information on safety issues raised by selank acetate administered to humans
  • Long-term effects of repeated use, including dependence or withdrawal, are not described in the sources reviewed
  • Status in the UK, Australia, Japan and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Selank is a stabilised analogue of tuftsin; in rodent and cell studies it altered expression of genes involved in GABA signalling and inflammation and changed brain BDNF levels.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
