Octreotide

Cyclic octapeptide analogue of somatostatin: D-Phe-Cys-Phe-D-Trp-Lys-Thr-Cys-Thr-ol, disulfide bridge Cys2-Cys7; contains D-amino acids and a C-terminal threoninol, so no standard one-letter sequence is given.

Non-standard residues or modifications: no plain one-letter sequence

Also: Octreotide acetate · Codes: SMS 201-995 · Brands: Sandostatin, Sandostatin LAR, Mycapssa

A synthetic eight-residue cyclic analogue of the hormone somatostatin, with a longer half-life than the native hormone. It is an approved prescription drug in the United States and Canada in short-acting and long-acting injectable forms, and in the United States as an oral delayed-release capsule. Labelled uses centre on acromegaly and on symptoms of carcinoid and other hormone-secreting neuroendocrine tumours.

02What the human evidence shows

E2

Acromegaly (biochemical control)

1published human RCTs
2published human trials
327registered studies
67with posted results

Injectable octreotide has been used in acromegaly since the late 1980s, largely on the basis of uncontrolled and open-label studies. The oral capsule was tested in a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled withdrawal trial (OPTIMAL, 56 participants) in people already controlled on injectable somatostatin analogues, and in an open-label randomised non-inferiority trial against injectables (MPOWERED). One adequately designed placebo-controlled RCT is cited here; the outcome is a biochemical marker (IGF-I), not tumour or survival outcomes.

  • [1] A higher proportion of participants on oral octreotide than on placebo kept IGF-I within the target range in a randomised withdrawal trial in adults with acromegaly

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “octreotide”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

Metastatic midgut neuroendocrine tumours (tumour growth control)

1published human RCTs
1published human trials
327registered studies
67with posted results

The PROMID trial randomised 85 treatment-naive patients with metastatic well-differentiated midgut neuroendocrine tumours to long-acting octreotide or placebo and reported a longer time to tumour progression with octreotide. No overall survival benefit was established in the primary report. This indication is not part of the US label wording for octreotide.

  • [2] Longer time to tumour progression with long-acting octreotide than with placebo in patients with metastatic midgut neuroendocrine tumours

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “octreotide”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

Carcinoid syndrome (diarrhoea and flushing)

1published human RCTs
1published human trials
34registered studies
16with posted results

The cited randomised trial compared long-acting and short-acting octreotide formulations in malignant carcinoid syndrome rather than octreotide against placebo; it was small and partly open-label. Symptom control with the two formulations was reported as similar. Placebo-controlled evidence for symptom relief with octreotide itself was not identified in this review.

  • [3] Long-acting and short-acting octreotide formulations gave comparable control of diarrhoea and flushing in patients with malignant carcinoid syndrome

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “octreotide carcinoid”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

Acute oesophageal variceal bleeding (off-label)

n/cpublished human RCTs
n/cpublished human trials

1with posted results

n/c: not counted here; see the cited review.

Octreotide is used off-label in acute variceal bleeding. A Cochrane systematic review of randomised trials of somatostatin analogues (somatostatin and octreotide) found no reduction in mortality and only a small effect on the need for transfusion, with trial quality and results varying. The number of octreotide-specific RCTs is not stated here because the review pooled both agents.

  • [4] Systematic review of randomised trials found no mortality reduction with somatostatin analogues in acute variceal bleeding

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “octreotide variceal”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S1Large long-term human exposure (post-marketing surveillance)
  • Gallbladder and biliary stone formation [5]
    Gallstones and gallbladder sludge are a labelled warning for somatostatin analogues; a case of intrahepatic stones during octreotide therapy for acromegaly has been reported.

04What is unknown

  • Whether octreotide improves overall survival in metastatic midgut neuroendocrine tumours has not been shown in the cited placebo-controlled trial
  • Placebo-controlled evidence for symptom relief in carcinoid syndrome with octreotide itself was not identified; the cited trial compared two octreotide formulations
  • Octreotide-specific effect estimates in acute variceal bleeding are not separable from somatostatin in the cited pooled review
  • Status in the UK, Australia, Japan and China was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

Octreotide binds somatostatin receptors, mainly subtypes 2 and 5, and in human studies lowers secretion of growth hormone, IGF-I and several gastrointestinal and pancreatic hormones.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
