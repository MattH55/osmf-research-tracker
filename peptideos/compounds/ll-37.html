LL-37

LLGDFFRKSKEKIGKEFKRIVQRIKDFLRNLVPRTES

37 residues cleaved from the C-terminus of the human cathelicidin precursor hCAP-18 (UniProt P49913, residues 134-170). Ropocamptide is the name used by its developer, Promore Pharma, for a topical synthetic LL-37 product.

Also: Cathelicidin LL-37, Human cathelicidin antimicrobial peptide, Ropocamptide

A 37-amino-acid antimicrobial peptide made naturally by human white blood cells and skin and mucosal cells, the only human member of the cathelicidin family. A synthetic version has been tested as a topical treatment for chronic leg and foot ulcers. Separately, it is sold on the gray market, often for injection, with claims for chronic infections, immune support and gut health. It is not approved as a medicine in any jurisdiction checked.

02What the human evidence shows

E3

Chronic venous leg ulcers unresponsive to standard care (topical synthetic LL-37)

2published human RCTs
2published human trials
20registered studies
4with posted results

A first-in-human randomised trial in 34 patients over four weeks reported faster healing-rate constants for the two lower concentrations than for placebo, but not for the highest. The larger phase 2b trial (HEAL LL-37, 148 patients, sponsored by the developer) found no significant improvement in healing compared with placebo in the full population; benefit in patients with large ulcers came from a post hoc subgroup analysis. The randomised evidence is conflicting and the larger trial was negative.

  • [1] No significant difference in ulcer healing versus placebo in 148 patients with venous leg ulcers; post hoc subgroup differences only
  • [2] Higher healing-rate constants than placebo at the two lower concentrations, not the highest, in 34 patients over four weeks

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “LL-37”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E3

Diabetic foot ulcers (topical synthetic LL-37)

1published human RCTs
1published human trials
1registered studies
0with posted results

One small randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in Jakarta treated mildly infected diabetic foot ulcers with LL-37 cream or placebo cream for four weeks. A granulation index increased more with LL-37; wound inflammatory cytokines and aerobic bacterial counts did not differ significantly between groups. No confirmatory trial was identified.

  • [3] Greater increase in granulation index than placebo over four weeks in patients with mildly infected diabetic foot ulcers; no difference in bacterial colonisation

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “LL-37 diabetic foot ulcer”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

E6

Systemic or chronic infections and immune support (injected LL-37, gray-market claims)

No human trial of any design has been published for this use.

0published human RCTs
0published human trials
20registered studies
4with posted results

LL-37 kills a range of bacteria in laboratory assays, with activity reduced for some organisms at physiological salt concentrations. In a mouse model of polymicrobial sepsis, intravenous LL-37 improved survival and lowered bacterial counts. No human trial of injected or oral LL-37 for any infection, for immune support or for gut health was identified. The only registered human trial of injected LL-37 identified was a four-patient intratumoural melanoma study, without published results.

  • [4] Intravenous LL-37 improved survival and reduced bacterial burden in mice with caecal ligation and puncture sepsis
  • [5] LL-37 showed antimicrobial activity against several bacterial species in vitro; some organisms were resistant in physiological salt

Registered-study counts: ClinicalTrials.gov intervention search “LL-37”, fetched 2026-10-10. Registered studies include trials of other uses; the gap between registered and reported is itself a finding.

03Safety signals

S3Limited human safety data

There are no adequate human safety data. The absence of reported harm is not evidence of safety.

04What is unknown

  • Human safety data come only from short topical use on ulcers; there are no published human safety or pharmacokinetic data for injected, oral or nasal LL-37
  • FDA cites nonclinical findings suggesting detrimental effects on male reproduction and protumorigenic effects in some tissues; their relevance to human use is not established
  • Endogenous LL-37 has been implicated in inflammatory skin disease such as rosacea and psoriasis; whether supplemental LL-37 affects these conditions is untested
  • Immunogenicity of injected synthetic LL-37 is unassessed
  • LL-37 is not named on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List; S0 is applied here because no approval by any regulator was identified, not because WADA has classified it by name
  • Regulatory status outside the US was not verified from regulator sources for this record

07Mechanism

LL-37 is a cationic, amphipathic peptide that disrupts bacterial membranes and binds bacterial lipopolysaccharide in vitro, and modulates inflammatory cytokine production by immune cells.

Mechanism is placed last on purpose: a plausible mechanism is not evidence that a compound works in people.
