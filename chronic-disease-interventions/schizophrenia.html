Research Tracker / Chronic Disease Interventions / Schizophrenia

Remission & chronicity

Spontaneous remission
Spontaneous full remission rare (<10%); after first episode, ~80% relapse within 5 years without maintenance antipsychotic; functional disability accumulates over time; 20% achieve good long-term outcome
Best-intervention remission
Long-acting injectable antipsychotics (LAIs): 30-50% reduction in relapse vs oral antipsychotics (PALIPERIDONE PALMITATE, aripiprazole monthly); clozapine for treatment-resistant: 30-60% response in patients who failed 2 other antipsychotics; critical period intensive early intervention (NAVIGATE program): 3x better employment, 30% less symptom burden at 2 years
Gap size
Large — LAIs chronically underused; clozapine for TRS grossly underused; early intervention services reach only a fraction of FEP patients
Primary barrier
practice
Clozapine is used in only 4% of TRS patients in the US despite clear efficacy (should be ~30%); LAIs offered to only 15-25% of patients despite superior relapse prevention vs oral; stigma and structural barriers delay first treatment by 1-2 years on average; many patients never receive coordinated specialty care
