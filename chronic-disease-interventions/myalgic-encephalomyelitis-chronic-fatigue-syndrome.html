Research Tracker / Chronic Disease Interventions / ME/CFS (Myalgic Encephalomyelitis / Chronic Fatigue Syndrome)

Remission & chronicity

Spontaneous remission
Full recovery without intervention: <5% of adult ME/CFS patients; partial improvement in ~40% over 5 years; post-COVID ME/CFS appears to have similarly low spontaneous recovery rate
Best-intervention remission
No disease-modifying treatment established; GET (graded exercise therapy) — former standard — shown harmful (PACE controversy, withdrawn from UK NICE guidance 2021); pacing (heart rate-based) prevents post-exertional malaise but not curative; low-dose naltrexone — ~30% improvement in small RCTs; rintatolimod (Ampligen) — Phase 3 failed FDA approval
Gap size
Very large — no effective treatment; historic misattribution to psychological causes delayed research by decades
Primary barrier
biological (no treatment) + framing (historic)
ME/CFS research has been systematically underfunded relative to disease burden (NIH funding $15M/year vs MS at $100M despite similar prevalence); PACE trial's GET/CBT approach adopted globally for years despite being harmful for many patients; NIH RECOVER initiative now represents largest-ever investment
